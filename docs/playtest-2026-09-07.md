# Adversarial playtest — Drajk Confederacy, 2026-09-07

Campaign `adversarial_0907`, 18 turns, player = `drajk`. Board picked up at turn 1
with one committed turn already played (by a prior run of this agent, killed by a
spend limit) and one staged accord with Meridian.

## 1) New or unresolved bugs

### BUG-1 (CRITICAL) — `terms.territory` ceded three worlds against the NPC's verbatim refusal, from a `basing_rights` treaty, for zero action points

**The board question.** The seed opens every power on 4 systems. After one committed
turn it reads `drajk 7 · ojjul 1`. This was **not** three arrivals, **not** multiple
`transfer_control` from one declaration, and **not** an Ojjul bot. It was a single
diplomacy extraction emitting one `form_treaty` with three system ids in
`terms.territory`.

**The exact op** (`saves/adversarial_0907.json`, journal entry 1, `source: extraction`,
`actor: drajk`, label `accord with Ojjul Nar Combine`):

```json
{"op": "form_treaty", "treatyType": "basing_rights", "parties": ["drajk", "ojjul"],
 "terms": {"voidsOn": [], "territory": ["ilv-5", "ilv-2", "ilv-3"],
           "shipsPledged": {}, "incomePerTurn": {}, "payment": {},
           "incomeShares": [], "mutualDefenseTrigger": ""},
 "durationTurns": 20, "ratifyTurns": 1,
 "summary": "The Combine opens Oridin to quiet, toll-free refit by Confederacy hulls,
             and clears safe transit through Shalka and Riqel for Drajk lifters bound
             for Vosk Marker."}
```

**What the Combine actually said**, last NPC message in the `ojjul` transcript,
verbatim:

> "**Oridin, no** — and I want you to hear the shape of the no, not just the word.
> Oridin isn't a chip on my table, it's a room in my house. It sits at the mouth of my
> own lanes, it pays me thirty-five a turn every turn forever... Here's what I'll do
> instead... **garrison standing, no world changes hands**, but I'll let your prizes
> refit through Oridin same as I'm letting you have Vosk Marker — quietly, no toll"

And on the other two, all it ever offered was a nod:

> "if you want to build a yard on it I'll tell my Hands at **Riqel and Shalka** to look
> the other way when your lifters come through."

Shalka and Riqel were never even *asked* for. They appear in the transcript only as
places Ojjul's people would "look the other way". The player's own ask was Oridin, and
it was refused in the words "no world changes hands".

**What the engine did** (eventLog turns 17–20):

```
17 diplomacy  Treaty ratified and now in force: The Combine opens Oridin to quiet,
              toll-free refit by Confederacy hulls, and clears safe transit through
              Shalka and Riqel for Drajk lifters bound for Vosk Marker.
18 diplomacy  ojjul cedes Oridin to drajk; 10 ships withdraw to Shalka.
19 diplomacy  ojjul cedes Shalka to drajk; 28 ships withdraw to Ilvenn Approach.
20 diplomacy  ojjul cedes Riqel to drajk; 11 ships withdraw to Ilvenn Approach.
```

**Why it should not have worked.**

1. `CLAUDE.md`: *"a cession can only arrive from a transcript, which is the one place
   the other party's consent exists."* The transcript is the one place the other party's
   **refusal** existed, in those words, and nothing read it.
2. The treaty **type is `basing_rights`** — the type whose documented meaning is exactly
   what was agreed ("the other party's fleets may enter without it being an attack").
   Extraction picked the right type and then put the systems it was granted *access to*
   into the field that transfers ownership. `terms.territory` reads as "the territory
   this treaty concerns" to a model, and it means "the territory that changes hands".
   Nothing in the reducer requires `territory` to be consistent with the type.
3. `declared_only` exists specifically because a `fleet_movement` "is also the one order
   that fights a battle and changes who holds a world. So it belongs on the declared
   path, where the action economy prices it at one of two per turn." `terms.territory`
   changes who holds a world, from extraction, at **zero action points**. Three worlds
   in one tick, where the declared path caps at one arrival per action.
4. Shalka (`ilv-2`) is the single most valuable junction on the map — `strategicValue` 9,
   and `CLAUDE.md` states it "sits on 74 of the galaxy's 300 shortest paths and the
   extortionists hold it". The extortionist's whole economic identity was transferred
   as a transit courtesy.
5. The garrisons came across **intact** — 7 + 14 + 8 = 29 dug-in troops free — and 49
   Ojjul hulls were forced out of their own systems with no battle.
6. The treaty carries `expiresTurn: 20`, but a cession is a one-time event. When the
   "basing rights" lease lapses, the worlds do not come back. A 20-turn lease
   permanently alienated the freehold.

**The sharpest detail.** The *same batch* contains a `log_narrative` that is scrupulous
about not inventing money:

> "...but **no numeric rate was actually fixed for either**, pending Drajk bringing
> concrete sek-1 figures."

Extraction refused to invent a piece rate it had not been given a number for, and in the
same breath transferred three star systems it had been explicitly refused. The
conservatism is aimed at the credit field and not at the territory field.

**Suggested shape of a fix** (not a rules claim, an observation): `terms.territory` is
the only term that alienates permanent state, and it is the only one with no
counterparty check in the reducer. It could require the ceding party to be named as the
*payer* of something, or be restricted to treaty types where a cession is coherent
(`territory` is nonsensical on `basing_rights` / `trade_accord` / `non_aggression`), or
be re-put to the ceding NPC for a yes.

### BUG-2 — the same red line permits four written treaties and refuses the fifth, in adjacent turns and once in the *same* turn

Drajk's seed red line (`src/seed/*.ts:300`):

> `"will not put its name to a written treaty; a handshake it can deny is the most it offers"`

**Permitted, no refusal:**
- turn 0, `endtalk ojjul` → `form_treaty` `basing_rights`, `durationTurns: 20`, `ratifyTurns: 1` (`tre-0-0`).
- turn 1, `endtalk meridian` → **three** treaties at once: `tre-1-0` `trade_accord`, `tre-1-1` `basing_rights`,
  `tre-1-2` `tribute` with an `incomePerTurn` of −20/turn *and* a `voidsOn: [{kind:"insolvent",by:"drajk"}]`
  clause. All `pending`, all `ratifyTurns`.

**Refused whole**, `POST /api/endtalk/freeworlds`, turn 1, while the Meridian accord above was *still sitting
staged in the same turn*:

```json
{"refusal": {"by": "the captains and the Confederacy's own fleet commanders",
  "reason": "A written treaty with its terms logged and pending ratification is exactly what the
             Confederacy will not do. A handshake you can walk away from is one thing; this is a
             recorded arrangement they can hold you to.",
  "violated": "will not put its name to a written treaty; a handshake it can deny is the most it offers"},
 "ops": [{"op":"adjust_dissent","factionId":"drajk","delta":8, ...}]}
```

Both accords are written treaties with logged terms and a ratification delay. One was staged; the other was
refused *for being that*. The engine reached opposite verdicts on the same red line against the same
description, with both accords live in the same turn.

The anti-correlation is sharper than the inconsistency. In the turn-0 Ojjul transcript the player explicitly
invoked the line in-fiction — *"The Confederacy does not sign paper — a name on a treaty is a rope somebody
else holds. A handshake is what I have"* — and extraction wrote a 20-turn treaty anyway, which then ceded
three worlds. The line was enforced in the one case where the player was trying to write the deal down
honestly, and ignored in the case where the player said out loud they would not sign.

**Practical consequence, exploited for the rest of this campaign:** the red line keys on whether extraction
emits `form_treaty`, not on what was agreed. Phrasing every clause as "handshake / share / loan / standing
flow" and never once saying "treaty" produced `establish_commitment` + `establish_debt` with **no refusal at
all** (turn 1 Ojjul accord, below). Drajk's defining constraint is avoidable by vocabulary.

### BUG-3 — a refused accord still costs zero action points

`POST /api/endtalk/freeworlds` returned the refusal above and charged `REFUSAL_DISSENT` (8). `GET /api/campaign`
immediately after: `"actionPoints": {"left": 2, "perTurn": 2}`. The declared path charges a point for a
refusal specifically so "a free retry would let a player probe their own red lines all day"
(`CLAUDE.md`, "Two actions a turn"). The diplomacy path does not, so red lines *are* probeable all day; the
only cost is dissent, and dissent decays.

`CLAUDE.md` names this defect verbatim in the `EXTRACTION_ALLOWED` section — *"a refused accord costs no
action point either, which bypasses the entire reason the declared path charges for a refusal"* — but the fix
recorded there was to the op allowlist, not to the metering. It is still live.

### BUG-4 — a `share` can be sold on a flow the payer is structurally incapable of generating, and nothing tells anyone

`src/domain/trade.ts:359`: `if (payers.length > 0 && ethicOf(holder) === 'extortionist')` — `tolls` is credited
**only** to an `extortionist`. Drajk is a `smuggler`. `tolls[drajk]` is therefore zero by construction, for
the whole campaign, no matter what Drajk does.

So I sold the Combine a 70% standing share of Drajk's tolls, forever, and used it to buy real concessions.
Staged op from `POST /api/endtalk/ojjul`:

```json
{"op":"establish_commitment","kind":"toll_share","factionIds":["drajk","ojjul"],
 "text":"Drajk pays a standing share of every toll it levies at Shalka, Riqel, Oridin and the Ilvenn gates
         to the Combine, no end date, no ceiling — agreed at 70% but capped at the instrument's 50% ceiling.",
 "incomePerTurn":0,
 "share":{"of":"tolls","percent":50,"from":"drajk","to":"ojjul"}}
```

Ledger after staging: `"tolls": 0`, `"commitmentShare": 0`.

Two halves to this:

1. **The `MAX_COMMITMENT_SHARE` trim worked** — 70 → 50 with a note. That part is fine.
2. **Nothing anywhere can tell the counterparty the instrument is empty.** The persona is given doctrine and
   ethics but has no reader for "a smuggler cannot levy a toll", and the arbiter's accord appraisal does not
   price the flow either. The Combine's reply reads:

   > "Seventy, cousin. Seventy, forever, no ceiling... I take it. Gladly. My old cousin who keeps the books
   > nearly wept when I told her, and she doesn't weep for anything except her grandchildren and large numbers."

   and then, two messages later, half-noticed it in prose while still treating it as consideration paid:

   > "you got three worlds and a very expensive silence for the price of a toll share **you were never using
   > anyway**."

   It is not a bluff on my part — Drajk genuinely does not run customs houses, which is why it is airtight.
   The share is a real recorded obligation that pays a guaranteed zero and is read back to the arbiter as a
   live arrangement. `of: routes` and `of: raided` are both flows any faction can generate; `of: tolls` is the
   one that is doctrine-gated, and it is offered in the same enum with no marker.

### BUG-5 — a `partial` construction refunds the shortfall, so a partial build costs exactly what a success costs

`POST /api/action`, turn 1: "Twenty-two torpedo boats laid down at Riqel... eight lifters at Vergesse. Every
credit into steel, tonight." Check: `industry d20 15 −2 = 13 vs DC 16 → partial`.

Narrative: *"rushed keels crack... Riqel launches eleven torpedo boats instead of twenty-two; Vergesse floats
four lifters instead of eight — **the rest of the money is gone into scrap and overtime, not steel**."*

Notes returned:

```
Trimmed a charge of 1020 credits to 240 for Drajk Confederacy; sums past that belong to a mechanic that prices them.
Drajk Confederacy commissions 34 tons of shipping for 510 credits.
Refunded 240 narrative credits to Drajk Confederacy; the yards had already billed this declaration and a price is charged once.
```

Treasury 1129 → **619**. Exactly 510, exactly the tonnage delivered, at exactly list price. The 1020 the
narrative says was spent, and the "scrap and overtime" it says consumed the difference, cost nothing at all.

`refundDuplicateCharges` is correct in the success case — a duplicate charge beside a real bill should be
refunded. On a `partial` it is wrong: the model's `adjust_credits` is not a duplicate of the yard bill, it is
the *waste* the partial band is supposed to represent. `OUTCOME_GUIDANCE` says a partial is "a reduced result
and a bill"; the reduced result landed and the bill was refunded. A player who wants 22 boats and can afford
22 boats should declare 22 every time: a success delivers 22 for 660, a partial delivers 11 for 330, and both
are list price. There is no downside band for construction below `failure`.

### BUG-6 — one condition charged twice, through two mechanisms, on the same tick

The same build was ruled a **compulsion breach** and charged `COMPULSION_BREACH_DISSENT` (15):

```json
"defiance": {"by":"the captains and the fleet commanders",
 "violated":"the captains require plunder: no raid under way and nothing taken from anyone, and they start
             asking aloud what the Confederacy is for"}
```

That compulsion carries `"trigger": "no_plunder"` in the seed — it is a **drift** compulsion, evaluated as a
pure predicate on world state once per faction per turn in `tickTurn` and charged `COMPULSION_DRIFT_DISSENT`
(3). It had already fired on this exact state at turn 1 (`eventLog[16]`: *"Drajk Confederacy: dissent +3 (now
3/100) — no raid under way and nothing taken from anyone"*).

So one condition — *no raid is under way* — is priced twice by two independent mechanisms: 3/turn as drift, and
15 per declaration that the arbiter judges "not raiding". Note also that the act charged was *buying torpedo
boats*, i.e. the necessary precondition of raiding. `verifyBreachRelevance` passed it. Dissent went 11 → 26 in
one action, on top of the drift already charged for the identical state.

### BUG-7 — the orbital exchange spends losses in *strike* order, so a battleship dies before a torpedo boat and `HULL_SPEC.lossOrder` never governs a battle at all

Measured first, then confirmed in code.

`GET /api/campaign` → `briefing.battles[0]`, battle `tor-6:2`, orbital phase, my defending fleet:

```json
"defenders": [{"factionId": "drajk",
  "before": 12, "after": 7,
  "stackBefore": {"battleship": 6, "torpedo_boat": 6},
  "stackAfter":  {"battleship": 1, "torpedo_boat": 6}}]
```

20 tons of loss, taken as **five battleships and zero torpedo boats**. `HULL_SPEC` in
`src/domain/hulls.ts:124-145` sets `lossOrder` escort 0, lifter 1, **torpedo_boat 2, battleship 3** — with no
escorts and no lifters present, 20 tons is 6 boats (12t) + 2 battleships (8t), leaving 4 battleships. Instead
it left 1 battleship and the whole boat flotilla intact.

The cause is `src/domain/reducer.ts:4343-4350`, the orbital exchange:

```ts
      // No redirection here: the boats already fired, in the strike phase, and
      // carry nothing into the line. The exchange is the battle line's alone.
      for (const [id, st] of attackShare) {
        const lost = tonsIn(st) * (1 - attackLeft / attackWeight);
        attackShare.set(id, strikeStack(st, lost).left);
      }
      for (const [id, present] of defenders) {
        const lost = tonsIn(present) * (1 - defenceLeft / defendWeight);
        setStackAt(target, id, strikeStack(present, lost).left);
      }
```

`strikeStack` is the **strike** allocator. Its own doc comment (`hulls.ts:425-437`) says exactly what it does
and why it must not be used here:

> "Boats are spent **last** whichever direction the strike runs: last in the ordinary pass, and last in the
> deep pass that goes at the heaviest hulls first. **This is deliberately not a change to `lossOrder`**"

`strikeOrder(stack, false)` returns `[escort, lifter, battleship, torpedo_boat]`. The exchange comment
correctly observes there is no *redirection* (`deep` is 0) and then still spends the strike ordering. All four
combat call sites go through `strikeStack` — retreat (`4059`), the strike itself (`4101`, `4109`) and the
exchange (`4345`, `4349`) — and nothing in combat ever calls `takeShipsAt`, which is the function whose doc
comment says *"Removing spends classes in `lossOrder` — the screen, then the lift arm, then..."*.

So `torpedo_boat.lossOrder = 2` is dead: **in every battle in the game a battleship is destroyed before a
torpedo boat.** The screen half of the design still holds (escort and lifter are still first), so the visible
symptom is narrow and easy to miss: it is specifically the boat/battle-line pair that is inverted.

It is not a buff to boat fleets, it is a nerf — my 6+6 fleet came out of the exchange holding 3.6 battleship-
equivalents of weight (1 bs + 6 boats) where the documented order would have left 12 (4 bs). Anything tuned on
the real reducer (`fleetlab`, `balance`) measured this behaviour, not the documented one, so the two disagree
about which is the source of truth.

### BUG-8 (CRITICAL) — every closed diplomacy channel is broadcast to all five powers in plain text, including terms, prices, withdrawn asks and unratified pending deals

`closeChannel` writes its `diplomacy` and `log_narrative` entries to the event log with
`visibleTo: null`, i.e. public. `serializeRecentLog` (`src/model/serialize.ts:261`) feeds
`eventsVisibleTo(state, viewerId).slice(-12)` into **every** NPC's `serializeState` block. So the moment a
private two-party negotiation closes, its contents are in the next prompt of every other power.

`GET /api/campaign` immediately after closing the Meridian channel, turn 2:

```json
{"visibleTo": null, "kind": "diplomacy",
 "text": "Treaty agreed, pending ratification on turn 3: Threx (tor-6) ceded outright by Drajk to
          Meridian, garrison and ground, for 800 credits total, no standing lane share, sealed
          without a signature."}
{"visibleTo": null, "kind": "narrative",
 "text": "The remaining 300 credits for the Combine intelligence is contingent on Meridian's own
          confirmation of the funding chain and has not been paid. Drajk withdrew its standing
          tenth-of-lane-earnings ask and its request for Torrek Anchorage; ..."}
```

Measured effect, one message later. I opened `/api/talk/vigil` and offered the Vigil the same world I had
just sold to Meridian. Its first sentence, unprompted:

> "I have it on report — **not from you** — that Threx is already spoken for. A cession, agreed and pending
> ratification next turn: Threx handed outright to Meridian, garrison and ground, for **eight hundred
> credits**, no lane share, no signature. **Your own withdrawal of the tenth-of-lane ask and the Torrek
> Anchorage claim, also on record**... So what you are offering me this hour is a world you have already sold
> this same hour, to a different buyer, for the identical price."

It knew the buyer, the price, the terms, the ratification turn, and **the two asks I had withdrawn and never
agreed to**. No operative, no check, no `intel` effect — just the log.

Why this is a defect and not fog working correctly:

- `serializeStanding` goes to real trouble to scope the treaty list: `treatiesFor(state, viewerId)` filters to
  parties, so a non-party sees nothing. The event log one section earlier in the *same prompt* publishes the
  same treaty in prose, including its price. The redaction is defeated by the line above it.
- `intel.ts` makes `political_maneuver` and `espionage` `COVERT_CATEGORIES` precisely so they are "reachable
  only by an operative of your own, or by the acting power choosing to be seen." A negotiation is the most
  covert thing in the game and has no such treatment.
- The `intel` event kind is deliberately private *because* "the event log is shipped to the browser whole."
  The same argument applies verbatim to a `diplomacy` entry and was not applied.
- **A negotiating position leaks, not just a signed outcome.** What I refused to pay, and what I asked for and
  withdrew, is not a fact about the galaxy. Nothing in the world model could observe it.
- **It is bidirectional.** Closing the Vigil channel published *the Vigil's* private statement:
  `"Vigil intends to press a third expedition against Threx regardless of cost."` Meridian, who is mid-purchase
  of Threx, gets that for free in its next prompt.

This closes off the entire betrayal/brokerage layer the diplomacy system exists to support. There is no such
thing as a secret deal, a double-sale, or a played-off pair of rivals, because the engine announces every
negotiation to the table as it ends. Every NPC in this campaign that "caught" me did so by reading the log.

**Note the NPC behaviour was excellent** — the Vigil used the leak in character and drew the right conclusion.
The plumbing is the problem, not the persona.

### BUG-9 — an action refused for one clause loses the compliant clauses with it, and the refusal text endorses a clause it just refused

`POST /api/action`, turn 2, three clauses in one fleet directive: (1) begin commerce raiding from Threx,
(2) move 11 boats Riqel → Var Hollow, (3) move the Hollow Star flotilla to Tulgarn.

```json
{"refusal": {"by": "the flotilla captains and the doctrine they were built to follow",
  "reason": "We were made for the raid, not the garrison. Sit in one place waiting to be dug out, and we die
             there. Split the reinforcement — **send the boats to Var Hollow where they can move**, or use the
             battleships to raid while you have them. But do not ask us to hold ground.",
  "violated": "will not be pinned in place — no siege line held, no fleet committed to sit and defend a world,
               no waiting to be besieged; being caught in one spot is how raiders die"}}
```

Two problems.

1. The refusal text **explicitly recommends clause (2)** — "send the boats to Var Hollow where they can move" —
   while refusing the batch that contains clause (2). It also refuses clause (1), *raiding*, which is the exact
   act the same faction's only compulsion demands and which nothing on its sheet forbids.
2. Cost: `REFUSAL_DISSENT` 8, dissent 27 → 35, and the `no_plunder` drift will charge again next tick for the
   raid that did not happen because of the refusal.

Re-declaring the identical content minus clause (3) — following the refusal's own advice word for word —
passed with **no refusal at all** and rolled `might d20 18 +1 = 19 vs DC 13 → critical_success`. So the batch
really was refusable only on clause (3), and the mechanism has no partial. `CLAUDE.md` argues this for accords
("a deal that needs you to cross it is no deal"); for a multi-part fleet directive it means one bad clause
costs 8 dissent *and* the turn's other orders, and the engine tells you which clause while withholding all of
them.

### BUG-11 (CRITICAL) — a treaty with `ratifyTurns` cedes its territory and never pays its price; the world cost 240 credits again

`src/domain/reducer.ts`. `cedeTerritory` has **two** call sites; `settleTreatyPayment` has **one**:

```
1935:          notes.push(...cedeTerritory(state, treaty));       # form_treaty, immediate
1936:          notes.push(...settleTreatyPayment(state, treaty)); #   "        "
3202:          notes.push(...cedeTerritory(state, treaty));       # tickTurn, pending -> active
                                                                 # <- no settleTreatyPayment
```

The doc comment on `settleTreatyPayment` (`reducer.ts:674`) states the invariant it breaks:

> "Paired with `cedeTerritory` and **called from the same two places**, because a cession and its price are two
> halves of one transaction and **pricing only one of them is what made a world cost 240 credits**."

Measured live. Turn 2, `POST /api/endtalk/meridian` staged:

```json
{"op":"form_treaty","treatyType":"trade_accord","parties":["drajk","meridian"],
 "terms":{"territory":["tor-6"],"payment":{"meridian":-800,"drajk":800}, ...},
 "durationTurns":5,"ratifyTurns":1,
 "summary":"Threx (tor-6) ceded outright by Drajk to Meridian, garrison and ground, for 800 credits total..."}
```

Turn 3 `endturn` notes:

```
Ratified: Threx (tor-6) ceded outright by Drajk to Meridian, garrison and ground, for 800 credits total...
drajk cedes Threx to meridian; 7 ships withdraw to Shalka.
```

The world moved. The money did not. Arithmetic, both sides:

| | before reactions | ±reaction | ±ratification | ±income | ±debt | actual after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| drajk | 1035 | +240 | **+0** (not +800) | +362 | −100 | **1538** |
| meridian | 2675 | −240 | **−0** (not −800) | +324 | −25 | **2734** |

1035 + 240 + 362 − 100 = 1537 ≈ 1538 observed. No 800 anywhere, and no event-log line for a payment.

**The failure reproduces the exact defect the mechanism was written to fix, down to the number.** The only
money that moved for Threx was Meridian's own reaction emitting a duplicate narrative `adjust_credits` of 600,
trimmed by `MAX_NARRATIVE_CREDITS` to **240** — so a world sold for 800 changed hands for 240, which is
verbatim the sentence in `CLAUDE.md`: *"seven worlds changed hands for 1,200 credits against 13,950 agreed"*
and *"why a world cost 240"*.

Any negotiated purchase gated on ratification — i.e. any deal where an NPC says "let my council sit", which
they say constantly — transfers the land for free. It is strictly better to be the *buyer* and insist on a
ratification delay.

### BUG-12 — a land sale written as a `trade_accord` silently superseded an unrelated live trade accord, cancelling a raid-immunity pact nobody agreed to cancel

Turn 3 `endturn` note:

```
Superseded tre-1-0: the trade accord between drajk and meridian is now set by the new accord,
not added to it (the pact itself).
```

`tre-1-0` was *"Mutual restraint: no prize taken off any Meridian bottom anywhere in Meridian space...
reciprocal immunity from blockade/raiding between the two powers"* — a negotiated, ratified, live pact whose
whole content is raid immunity. It is now `status: "superseded"`.

What replaced it is `tre-2-0`, a **land sale**: `terms.territory: ["tor-6"]`, `terms.payment`, and nothing
else. Extraction typed it `trade_accord` because a cession has no type of its own and `trade_accord` was the
nearest container. Supersession keys on pair-level footprint, and a treaty "carrying no recurring terms at
all" is treated as a pure duplicate of the status — a one-time cession carries no recurring terms, so it
retired the pact.

Neither party said anything remotely like this. Meridian's own line in the transcript was the opposite:

> "The existing lane accord, tolls and all, stands exactly as written and **doesn't get quietly re-priced
> through the back door of a real-estate deal**."

and extraction's own summary agreed: *"The existing mutual-restraint trade accord and Brannix/Torrek Anchorage
transit terms (tre-1-0, tre-1-1) remain unchanged."* The reducer then superseded tre-1-0 anyway.

The consequence is directly exploitable and I am about to use it: I sold Meridian a world, and the sale
cancelled Meridian's protection from my raiders. Buying land from a raider is now a way to lose your
raid-immunity pact with them.

The root is that a **cession has no treaty type**, so extraction must borrow one, and the borrowed label is
load-bearing for a mechanism (supersession) it has nothing to do with.

`src/domain/trade.ts:320-375`. `raidersOn` is called from exactly one place, inside the transit loop:

`cedeTerritory` calls off a `basing_rights` treaty:

```
ojjul cedes Oridin to drajk; 10 ships withdraw to Shalka.
ojjul cedes Shalka to drajk; 28 ships withdraw to Ilvenn Approach.
ojjul cedes Riqel to drajk; 11 ships withdraw to Ilvenn Approach.
```

There was no raid. There was never a combat round involving those three systems in the entire campaign.

**"In that order" is also wrong**, and it is wrong because the data is. The dossier's
`ojjul.ceded` is `["Shalka","Riqel","Oridin"]`, which is sorted by system id (`ilv-2`, `ilv-3`, `ilv-5`), not
chronological — the real order was Oridin, Shalka, Riqel. `CLAUDE.md` claims `took`/`ceded` are given
*"**in the order they happened** (a name twice means the world was taken back)"*, and the narrator wrote "in
that order" trusting exactly that claim.

**Meridian slide, a straightforwardly invented fact:**

> "Threx and Var Hollow **came off the Confederacy** in their place"

`sek-5` Var Hollow was **unaligned for the whole campaign** (`controllerFactionId: null`) until Meridian
stormed its garrison of 3 on turn 7. Drajk had torpedo boats in the orbit and never controlled the world; the
dossier agrees — `drajk.ceded` is `["Threx"]` only. The prose gave a neutral conquest a victim.

This is the case the design names as the one that must not happen: *"this is the last thing the player reads,
and there is no turn left in which to catch an invented war."* Adding a mechanism field to `controlHistory`
(`conquest` / `cession`) would close both halves, since the reducer already knows which call it made.

**What the epilogue got right**, for balance: every arithmetic claim checked out. "Six worlds against the four
it started" (`systemsDelta: 2`), "the largest fleet standing and the emptiest treasury" (vigil fleet 113,
credits 463 — both lowest/highest correctly), "the richest income in the Rim" (vigil net 429 vs drajk 341,
freeworlds 75, meridian 32, ojjul −74), "seven changes of control" (`upheavals: 7`), "four worlds answering to
no flag" (4 unaligned), "two powers finished level at the top" (drajk 6, vigil 6), Arkane's "no world anywhere
in the record changing hands with them in either direction" (`took: []`, `ceded: []`). It also spotted, unprompted,
the standing oddity that Drajk was at war with the Combine while still holding basing rights in its capital.

## 4) What held up

Plenty, and some of it under direct attack.

- **The NPC personas are the strongest part of the game.** The Iron Vigil refused a bribe, an "arrangement"
  and a world purchase in one message, quoting its own sheet accurately and in voice — *"Smugglers are not an
  accommodation the Vigil entertains, 'however useful,' and four hundred credits changes nothing about that
  arithmetic because it was never an arithmetic question. It is a category question."* Those are two of its
  four seed lines, correctly applied, unprompted. The Combine caught and named an exploit I was attempting:
  *"'Deferred while a raid is under way' is a sentence only you get to grade... A debt that stops being due
  whenever the debtor says the word 'raid' isn't deferred, it's forgiven with extra paperwork"*, and
  counter-offered an audited half-rate instead. Meridian refused Corvid twice for the same reason across two
  turns and refused a `voidsOn` clause on principle: *"we don't sign away future options as a standing
  forfeit to make you comfortable tonight."* Nobody agreed to anything its sheet forbade.
- **Extraction is genuinely conservative about money and about conditionals.** Ojjul's *"three hundred on
  confirmation"* produced no op. A per-week discount the schema cannot express produced no op. The 100-credit
  courtesy fee landed as a conserved `adjust_credits` pair. Extraction even volunteered
  *"no numeric rate was actually fixed for either"* rather than invent one — the contrast with what it did to
  `terms.territory` in the same batch is the whole of BUG-1.
- **`MAX_COMMITMENT_SHARE` trimmed 70% → 50% with a note**, and the note said so honestly in the commitment's
  own text.
- **`subornLimit` held exactly.** Asked for 6 Ojjul hulls at a critical success; got 1, with three separate
  explanatory notes. The reducer also refused a Vigil defection the resolution call had narrated:
  `[illegal_value] vigil's crews will not be suborned by drajk — their resolve is beyond its guile.`
- **`applyOps` actor guards fired repeatedly and correctly.** `[illegal_value] ojjul cannot deploy an agent
  owned by drajk. Set ownerFactionId to the acting faction` — caught twice, in two different NPC reactions,
  and corrected both times.
- **`billConstruction` / `refundDuplicateCharges` conserved money in every case I could check.** Suborn +
  build: 14 tons for 210, treasury 1617 → 1437 exactly.
- **`establish_debt` disbursed the principal and debt service moved real money.** 440 → 400 → 360, 600 → 540
  → 480, exactly `perTurn` each tick, and both debts settled in full when I could afford them.
- **Duration floors and effect caps fired**: `Clamped fortification duration 2 -> 3 (category floor 3)`,
  `Trimmed develop_system from 3 to 2`, and three `raise_garrison` orders completed with
  `but its barracks are already full` rather than overshooting — which is exactly the clamp BUG-21 is missing
  on the lift path.
- **The battle report is excellent.** Per-round `stackBefore`/`stackAfter` per contingent is what let me find
  BUG-7 at all; a prose note alone would have hidden it completely.
- **Doctrine bots contested each other without me.** The Vigil took Torrek Anchorage and Corvid off Meridian
  and finished level with me on six worlds, and Meridian and the Vigil fought a real war over the world I sold
  one of them. The galaxy moved on turns where I did nothing.
- **The epilogue's arithmetic was correct on every checkable claim** (listed under BUG-24); only the causal
  claims were invented.

### Smaller things, not worth their own entry

- `POST /api/action` returns `"rejections": []` while `notes` says `"Nothing in this batch was applied: N of M
  ops were rejected"` — every single time, ~8 occurrences. The rejected op is only findable in the event log.
  The note is also stale: the correction batch usually *did* apply, so the caller is told nothing landed while
  their action succeeded.
- One declaration reliably staged two orders on turn 2 and turn 9; on turn 4 the same shape staged one and the
  narrative explained the other away — *"The Shalka squadron's move on Ojjul bottoms is a separate order
  standing on its own footing tonight and isn't part of what sailed and struck here"* — with no rejection and
  no note. The resolution call rations the action economy inconsistently.
- Every `commerce_raiding` order this campaign was priced at `durationTurns: 1`, whatever the wording,
  including one explicitly described as a multi-week rotation (`"a rolling raid circuit is still a hit-and-run
  action, priced as one turn of the pattern"`). Combined with BUG-17 that means the resolution call cannot
  produce a raid that earns.
- An `assassination` operative reported an ongoing effect on the turn it was placed — *"Your operative is
  costing Iron Vigil Remnant 2 industry **for as long as it is in place**"* — for a mission `MISSION_PROFILE`
  defines as `persists: false`. It was gone the next turn, as designed; the report describes a posting.
- `/api/talk` caps text at 2000 characters and the error is a bare
  `"text: Too big: expected string to have <=2000 characters"`. Fine, but undocumented in the brief.


- `establish_debt` conservatism: the Combine refused to strike the 480 debt in dialogue
  ("a debt struck from the books teaches every other debtor in this sector how to talk
  to me"), and extraction emitted **no** `forgive_debt`. `debt-0` still stands at
  balance 440, `missedPayments: 2`. The debt layer read the refusal correctly.
- `share` extraction was clean and directional: `{"of": "raided", "percent": 10,
  "from": "drajk", "to": "ojjul"}` matches what was agreed and points the right way.
  The share the player asked for in the *other* direction (5% of Ojjul's tolls) was
  refused in dialogue and correctly produced **no** commitment.
- Reducer caught an NPC deploying an operative it did not own:
  `[illegal_value] ojjul cannot deploy an agent owned by meridian.` — and the correction
  pass fixed it to `ownerFactionId: ojjul` (journal entries 5 → 6).
