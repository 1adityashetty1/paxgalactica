# Playtest 2026-10-18 — adversarial, intent ops and breaches

The adversarial-player agent played the Meridian Trade Authority for 12 turns
through `scripts/pt.mjs`, against `claude/intent-ops` at 3e5119e (journal
version 25). The run attacked the seven intent-ops changes and the breach
family. It cost $18.84 of model spend. The subscription hit its session limit
midway through turn 7, and the run resumed after the reset. A ransom accord on
turn 7 is confounded by an appraisal call that failed under the limit.

Final position: 8 worlds (from 4), 1,208 credits, net 537 a turn, dissent 0 and
heat 9. Meridian was still at war with the Vigil. The epilogue was generated
normally and ranked Meridian "foremost" and "ascendant". The other powers ended
on: Vigil 4, Ojjul 5, Free Worlds 5, Drajk 3.

The raw responses are in `saves/pt18-out-*.json` (gitignored) and the session
is in `saves/pt-session-1018.json`. The campaign replays byte-identically from
its journal.

**Verified** means the cause was found in the code or the journal; everything
else is the agent's report with the evidence it cited.

## A. Severe

| | finding | severity |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Conceding an ultimatum handed the issuer's own world to the target.** Meridian demanded Kalzir (`dem-8-0`) and took it by force on turn 9. At the deadline the Vigil "gave way", and `concedeDemand` formed cession `tre-10-0`. `cedeTerritory` has no direction: it moves a named world from whichever party holds it to the other. So Meridian ceded Kalzir to the Vigil, with its fixture and its notable, and its 7 ships withdrew. The epilogue lists Kalzir under Meridian's cessions. A demand whose terms the issuer already holds should lapse as met, and a cession should name its ceder. **Verified.** | bug |

## E. Corrected on checking

- **Upkeep is charged on ships under way.** The agent read the briefing and reported transit as unbilled: 120 upkeep against about 283 tons on turn 12. In fact `fleetTonsOf` counts transit, and income is paid before any order moves. What is wrong is the **briefing's ledger**: `tickTurn` computes it after arriving fleets have left `pendingOrders` and before they land, so it leaves them out. Replayed, the briefing read 122 where the world held 156 tons on turn 2, and 120 against 238 on turn 12. **Verified**; a display bug. A zero-length move (B3) therefore does not dodge upkeep.
