# Designs: running dark, intel with memory, speculative trade

The three ideas from `brainstorm-2026-10-02.md` (§3.4, §3.1, §4.2) that are
ready to build, worked out against the code as it stands after #54 and #55.
Each section says what exists today, what the design adds, where it touches the
rest of the game, how it would be measured, and the decisions still open, with
a recommendation for each.

Two things turned up while reading the code for these, and both change the
designs:

- **The fog hides four things and ships the rest whole.** `worldAsSeenBy`
  redacts pending orders, the event log, operatives and Rim events. Credits,
  every hull, **every treaty, commitment, debt and loan**, every asset and the
  base stats all reach the browser for every power. Some of that is public by
  design (fleet totals, treasuries). Some contradicts what `CLAUDE.md` says: a
  commitment is described as *"not public business"*, and the Treaties panel
  shows only your own, yet the payload carries everyone's.
- **The client computes ledgers from the redacted world.** The Trade tab and
  the Factions panel call `ledgerFor` in the browser. A raid on you is a rumour
  in your view, so `routeEarnings` there never subtracts it, and your own route
  income reads higher than the tick pays. The briefing's ledger is computed on
  the server and is right. `CampaignView.effective` already exists for exactly
  this mismatch, for stats.

---

## 1. Running dark — *Starsector's transponder*

### What exists today

- `commerce_raiding` is a **covert** category, so the victim and onlookers see
  a rumour rather than the order — but `OrderRumour` carries `factionId`, so
  the rumour names the raider.
- **Every turn the tick logs it anyway**, by name and in public: *"Drajk
  Confederacy continues to raid shipping at Oridin."* It also charges
  `INTERDICTION_DISPOSITION_COST` (4) with the victim, `PIRACY_REPUTATION_COST`
  (2) with every onlooker (smugglers exempt), and `HEAT_PER_RAID` (2) unless a
  letter of marque licenses it.
- So a raid is covert in name only. There is no way to raid without being known
  to be raiding.
- A raider lurks a jump out, so its hulls are in `system.ships` on its own or a
  neighbouring world. A listener hears a raid within `EMISSION_RANGE` (2) — in
  full, owner included.

### The design

`issue_order` of type `commerce_raiding` gains `dark: boolean`, default false.

**What changes while it is dark:**

| | an open raid | a dark raid |
|---|---|---|
| the rumour | names the raider | *"unknown raiders"* |
| the tick's log line | names the raider, public | *"raiders take shipping at Oridin"*, no name |
| bounty payouts in the log | name the claimant | name nobody; the claimant's own copy does |
| victim's regard, onlookers' regard, heat | charged every turn | **owed**, not charged |
| a listener in range | hears the raid and its owner | hears the raid, not the owner, and helps unmask it |
| prizes | `RAID_SHARE` | `DARK_RAID_YIELD` (0.5) of that — staying out of sight costs half the prizes |

The costs are **deferred, not waived**: the order keeps a running tally of what
an open raid would have cost (`owed: { regard, reputation, heat }`). A dark raid
that ends without being caught costs nothing — that is the reward. One that is
caught costs double.

**No anonymous hulls.** The brainstorm assumed the fog would have to learn an
anonymous stack. It does not: the hulls a jump out are visible, and visible
hulls are suspicion, not proof — everyone can see the Confederacy has a
squadron next to Oridin, and nobody can prove whose raiders took the cargo.
Hiding the owner of a stack would break the property the fog was built around
(`fleet_movement` is public, so every power's fleet total is exact from the
redacted view) and every battle report that names its sides.

**Only raids.** A blockade sits on the system — its ships in orbit are the
evidence. A fleet movement is the one order that is public by design.

**Unmasking.** Each turn, each power the dark raid robbed rolls its own d20
(seeded on the order and the victim):

```
d20 + victim's guile modifier + 4 per listener of its within range
    + 4 if it has a watcher on the raider's ground
  ≥ DARK_DC (16) + raider's guile modifier
```

At equal guile and no help that is 25% a turn; with a listener in range, 45%.
Those numbers are a starting point to sweep, not a choice.

**What unmasking gives the victim: proof.** Recommended over the brainstorm's
"full cost, doubled, at once":

- The victim's own regard drops by the doubled owed amount immediately — it
  knows now.
- The raid goes open from that turn and is charged like any other.
- The victim is handed a **dossier with a new secret kind, `dark_raid`**
  (subject the raider, ref the order). That is the existing secrets machinery:
  `publish_dossier` charges the raider the doubled owed reputation with every
  onlooker and the doubled owed heat; `blackmail` turns it into a strong hook;
  holding it does nothing.
- The secret stays live while that raider has a raid on the victim, and for
  `DARK_PROOF_TURNS` (6) after its last prize — after that it is old news, like
  any other proof.

This makes the victim decide what being robbed in the dark is worth, and the
bots already decide it well: `useProof` publishes against an enemy and
blackmails anyone else.

### Bots

Whether a raid runs dark is the raider's own call, made per raid. A
model-driven Drajk makes it in its reaction like any other order; its bot weighs
the trade a player would — half the prizes, against the standing and heat an
open raid costs. The bot's exact rule is settled when it is built and measured.

### UI and prompts

- Orders panel: the raider's own raid is marked dark, with the owed tally.
- Rumours: *"unknown raiders at Oridin"*.
- The victim's Trade tab: *"lost to raiders: N"*, attributed or not — which needs
  the ledger fix below.
- `resolution.md`: how to declare a raid dark (*"raid them quietly, no colours"*)
  and that it takes fewer prizes. `appraisal.md`: price it as `guile`.
  `reaction.md`: an NPC robbed in the dark suspects and cannot prove, and does
  not name the raider as fact.

### Prerequisite: serve the true ledger

The victim can only see what it lost if its ledger is the server's. Add the
player's ledger to `CampaignView.effective` beside the stats and span, and have
the Trade tab and the player's Factions row read it. Rivals' rows stay
estimates from the redacted world, which is honest — they are.

### Measurement

The harness, with and without dark raids: Drajk's heat, the Combine's regard
for Drajk, how often a dark raid is unmasked and by whom, how often the proof
is published against blackmailed, and the board at 30 and 100 turns.

### Journal

No old journal can hold a dark raid, and open raids are untouched, so no
version pin — except that anonymising bounty-payout log lines must apply to
dark raids only.

### Open decisions

1. **What unmasking does.** Proof the victim chooses how to use (recommended),
   or the brainstorm's immediate doubled scandal.
2. **The prize penalty.** Decided: `DARK_RAID_YIELD` 0.5. Dark takes half
   the prizes, so it is a real trade against the heat and standing it saves.

**Size: M.**

## 2. Intel with memory — *Stellaris*

### What exists today

- **What is actually hidden** — the only things a level could reveal:
  - orders in the eight secret categories (four covert: espionage,
    counter-intelligence, political manoeuvre, commerce raiding; four yard
    programmes: refit, retooling, capital-ship construction, industrial
    conversion), which reach others as a rumour of *whose, where, how long, how
    far along*;
  - unexposed operatives;
  - covert stat debuffs on a rival;
  - a rival's secrets, which a watcher can dig up.
- **What is not hidden**, so a threshold cannot reveal it: fleet totals (exact by
  design, from public movements) and treasuries (credits ship whole). The
  brainstorm's first and third thresholds are both already free.
- **Sight is a snapshot.** Burn the watcher and the programme it saw goes back
  to being a rumour on the next view. `CLAUDE.md` names this as the known
  simplification.

### The design

Two halves, and the first is most of the value.

**Memory — last-seen rows.** Each tick, for every power, every order it sees in
full that is neither its own nor public is remembered as a dated snapshot:
type, label, origin, target, progress, duration and the turn seen. When that
order drops back to a rumour, the row stays, greyed: *"last seen turn 7: capital
ship construction at Vantic, 2 of 5"*.

- **No id in the payload.** The server keeps the order id to match the row to
  the live order; `worldAsSeenBy` strips it, by the rumour's reasoning.
- **Rows age out**, after `INTEL_MEMORY_TURNS` (8) or when the order is seen
  again. Never when the order completes — removing the row then would tell the
  viewer it had completed.
- **For every power**, not only the player, because the NPC prompts read
  `ordersVisibleTo` and should remember too. The bots stay fog-clean: they read
  no rival orders, remembered or live.

**Intel — a level per pair.** `intel[viewer][subject]`, 0 to 100, on
`WorldState`.

| grows each turn by | |
|---|---|
| each watcher of yours at work on their ground | +6 |
| each listener of yours on or within range of their ground | +3 |
| a live trade accord with them | +2 |
| each battle you fought them | +5 |
| each of their operatives you take, once | +10 |
| decay | −2 |

Thresholds, retargeted at what is actually hidden:

| level | what it reveals about that power |
|---|---|
| 20 | its rumours carry the order's **type** — *"a capital ship programme at Vantic"* rather than *"something under way"* |
| 40 | its rumours carry **what the work will deliver** — hulls, a fixture, a developed world — and its target |
| 60 | its unexposed operatives **on your ground** are visible to you, and fail your exposure roll 1 in 20 more often |
| 80 | your watchers on its ground dig its secrets at +3 |

### Where it touches the rest

- `observeOrders` takes the viewer's intel on the order's owner to decide how
  much of a rumour to show; `OrderRumour` gains optional `type` and `delivers`.
- `agentsVisibleTo` adds operatives of a power you hold 60+ on, on your worlds.
- NPC prompts: last-seen lines in the orders block, and the intel level in each
  faction row.
- UI: an intel bar per power on the Factions panel, with the threshold names;
  last-seen rows greyed in the Orders panel; upgraded rumours.

### Measurement

The harness can measure only accrual — the bots read no rival orders, so
nothing they do changes with intel. The value is the player's and the NPC
prompts'. Measure the levels each pair reaches over 30 and 100 turns, and check
the thresholds are reached at all (the bots run watchers, so they should be).

### Journal

New state and new tick work, so `JOURNAL_VERSION` 15 with `LegacyRules.intel`.
The tick would compute `observeOrders` once per power per turn; not yet
measured, so it wants a `pnpm perf` run.

### Open decisions

1. **Whether intel should also hide what is public now** — rival treasuries,
   treaties, debts. Recommended **no**: that is a fog rework of every panel. Fix
   the actual leaks (other powers' treaties, commitments, debts and loans in the
   payload) on their own, and keep intel about the hidden things.
2. **The thresholds above**, or a different ladder.
3. **Memory for every power** (recommended, for the prompts) or the player only.

**Size: M+.**

## 3. Speculative trade — *Traveller*

### What exists today

- A freighter earns only on lanes crossing **unaligned** ground, where
  `FREIGHTER_LANE_WEIGHT` (2) doubles its claim on the hop. Nowhere else.
- `worldType` (seven kinds, three or four worlds each) decides a world's stat
  bonus and nothing about trade.
- Goods exist as two things: each power's own commodity (2 a turn, worth 10 to
  anyone else) and the "stuff" assets — ore, salvage, contraband, relic — that
  attempts produce, which shortages raise ×1.5 for three turns.
- The brainstorm called this *"micro-scale for a grand-strategy turn"*, which is
  the problem the design has to solve.

### The design

**Grand-strategy shaped: a standing run, not a shipment.** One declaration sets
up a `trade_run` between two worlds, and it runs on its own until cancelled, the
way a raid does: freighters sail, sell, sail back, buy, repeat. The player
decides the route once.

**A closed table of goods by world type**, one made and one wanted per type,
so every type has a buyer:

| world type | makes | wants |
|---|---|---|
| industrialmoon | machine parts | food |
| gasgiant | fuel | machine parts |
| oceanic | food | fuel |
| arid | ore | water |
| ice | water | luxuries |
| earthlike | luxuries | ore |
| earthnight | data | luxuries |

(Names to be set in the setting's voice; the shape is what matters.)

**Price**, per unit, derived and never stored:

```
TRADE_BASE (10)
  × 0.5 where it is made, 1.5 where it is wanted, 1.0 otherwise
  × the shortage factor, when the Rim has made it scarce
  × saturation at the selling world
```

**Saturation is the bound.** Each load sold at a world drops what that world
pays for that good by 10%, recovering 5% a turn. One route worked hard pays
less every trip, so trade spreads across the map instead of becoming one
perpetual engine. It is the one piece of new state:
`WorldState.markets[systemId][good]`.

**Where the money goes:**

- Buying pays the source world's holder, or nobody if it is your own world or
  unaligned — the goods leave the economy.
- Selling is paid by the market, so it **mints** the margin. The selling
  world's holder takes `BASE_TOLL_RATE` (12%) of the sale as a tariff when it is
  not the trader.
- Tolls already apply on the way: each hop held by a power that tolls the trader
  takes its rate of the cargo's value.

**Capacity.** A freighter carries `CARGO_PER_FREIGHTER` (6 units — the same
figure as a lifter's carry). Ten freighters at a margin of 10 a unit earn 600 a
delivery; on a four-jump route (eight turns a round trip) that is about 75 a
turn against 30 a turn of upkeep, before saturation, tolls and raids. Close to
what a hub pays, which is the right order of magnitude; to be swept.

**Risk on the road**, all from mechanics that exist:

- A raid on a hop the run crosses takes `RAID_SHARE` of the cargo — unless
  escorts in the run match the raider's tonnage. That gives escorts a peacetime
  job: convoy duty.
- A blockade or an ion storm on the path halts the run at the previous world,
  as a fleet movement halts (smugglers run both).
- A run cannot be sent to a power you are at war with; if war breaks out under
  way, the cargo is seized as an asset when it arrives.

**The order.** `trade_run` is a new order type: a movement (its duration is its
jumps, computed, never estimated), public like a fleet movement, carrying only
freighters and escorts, and it arrives as a guest — it contests no income and
fights no battle. A declared action; the arbiter prices it `industry`.

**Not in the first version: warehousing.** True speculation — buy now, hold,
sell into a shortage — needs cargo as assets at a world plus buy and sell ops.
The standing run gets most of the value with one order; shortages still change
what a delivery fetches when it arrives.

### Bots

Meridian, the free trader with the most freighters, runs standing routes between
its own worlds and its friends'. The others run one only where a margin is
obvious. Escort assignment follows from the raid risk on the path.

### Ledger and UI

- Sales are lump sums on arrival, so like hull bounties they are not a ledger
  rate. Each arrival is a briefing line, and the Trade tab keeps a per-route
  running average.
- Trade tab: what each world makes and wants and what it pays now, runs under
  way with ETA and expected margin. The map draws runs as it draws fleets.

### Measurement

- `tests/balance.test.ts` holds the lane share of income between 20% and 50%;
  trade-run income must be its own figure, or that bound means something new.
- Sweep `TRADE_BASE`, the saturation step and `CARGO_PER_FREIGHTER` on the
  harness: does Meridian get richer, does the board move, and do raiders now
  have convoys to hit?

### Journal

No old journal holds a trade run, and markets default to unsaturated, so no
version pin.

### Open decisions

1. **A standing run** (recommended) or one-shot shipments.
2. **Cargo carried by the order** (recommended for now) or warehoused as assets,
   for speculation over time.
3. **The sale mints, with a tariff to the holder** (recommended) or the holder
   pays, which conserves money but makes the bots agree to every delivery.
4. **Escorts protect convoys** (recommended) or raids always take their share.

**Size: M+**, heavier than the brainstorm's estimate: a new movement order, a
market, saturation and risk on the road.

---

## Suggested order

1. **Running dark**, with the served-ledger fix. Smallest, and heat is already
   there for it to defer.
2. **Fix the fog leaks** — other powers' treaties, commitments, debts and loans
   in the payload — before intel, so intel is built on a fog that holds.
3. **Intel with memory.**
4. **Speculative trade**, last: largest, and running dark makes raiding its
   convoys a real question first.
