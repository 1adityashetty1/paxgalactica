# Designs: running dark, intel with memory, speculative trade

*Status, 2026-10-05:* running dark, the fog leaks and the served ledger are
built (see `CLAUDE.md`, "Running dark" and "And everyone's paper"). Intel
with memory and speculative trade are not; both sections were revised after
#57 against the code as it now stands, with a build order for each.

The three ideas from `brainstorm-2026-10-02.md` (§3.4, §3.1, §4.2) that are
ready to build, worked out against the code as it stands after #54 and #55.
Each section says what exists today, what the design adds, where it touches the
rest of the game, how it would be measured, and the decisions still open, with
a recommendation for each.

Two things turned up while reading the code for these, and both changed the
designs. Both are fixed now:

- **The fog hides four things and ships the rest whole.** `worldAsSeenBy`
  redacts pending orders, the event log, operatives and Rim events. Credits,
  every hull, **every treaty, commitment, debt and loan**, every asset and the
  base stats all reach the browser for every power. Some of that is public by
  design (fleet totals, treasuries). Some contradicts what `CLAUDE.md` says: a
  commitment is described as *"not public business"*, and the Treaties panel
  shows only your own, yet the payload carries everyone's.
- **The client computes ledgers from the redacted world.** The Trade tab and
  the Factions panel call `ledgerFor` in the browser. A raid on you is a rumour
  in your view, so `routeEarnings` there never subtracts it, and your own route
  income reads higher than the tick pays. The briefing's ledger is computed on
  the server and is right. `CampaignView.effective` already exists for exactly
  this mismatch, for stats.

## 1. Running dark — *Starsector's transponder*

### What exists today

- `commerce_raiding` is a **covert** category, so the victim and onlookers see
  a rumour rather than the order — but `OrderRumour` carries `factionId`, so
  the rumour names the raider.
- **Every turn the tick logs it anyway**, by name and in public: *"Drajk
  Confederacy continues to raid shipping at Oridin."* It also charges
  `INTERDICTION_DISPOSITION_COST` (4) with the victim, `PIRACY_REPUTATION_COST`
  (2) with every onlooker (smugglers exempt), and `HEAT_PER_RAID` (2) unless a
  letter of marque licenses it.
- So a raid is covert in name only. There is no way to raid without being known
  to be raiding.
- A raider lurks a jump out, so its hulls are in `system.ships` on its own or a
  neighbouring world. A listener hears a raid within `EMISSION_RANGE` (2) — in
  full, owner included.

### The design

`issue_order` of type `commerce_raiding` gains `dark: boolean`, default false.

**What changes while it is dark:**

| | an open raid | a dark raid |
|---|---|---|
| the rumour | names the raider | *"unknown raiders"* |
| the tick's log line | names the raider, public | *"raiders take shipping at Oridin"*, no name |
| bounty payouts in the log | name the claimant | name nobody; the claimant's own copy does |
| victim's regard, onlookers' regard, heat | charged every turn | **owed**, not charged |
| a listener in range | hears the raid and its owner | hears the raid, not the owner, and helps unmask it |
| prizes | `RAID_SHARE` | `DARK_RAID_YIELD` (0.5) of that — staying out of sight costs half the prizes |

The costs are **deferred, not waived**: the order keeps a running tally of what
an open raid would have cost (`owed: { regard, reputation, heat }`). A dark raid
that ends without being caught costs nothing — that is the reward. One that is
caught costs double.

**No anonymous hulls.** The brainstorm assumed the fog would have to learn an
anonymous stack. It does not: the hulls a jump out are visible, and visible
hulls are suspicion, not proof — everyone can see the Confederacy has a
squadron next to Oridin, and nobody can prove whose raiders took the cargo.
Hiding the owner of a stack would break the property the fog was built around
(`fleet_movement` is public, so every power's fleet total is exact from the
redacted view) and every battle report that names its sides.

**Only raids.** A blockade sits on the system — its ships in orbit are the
evidence. A fleet movement is the one order that is public by design.

**Unmasking.** Each turn, each power the dark raid robbed rolls its own d20
(seeded on the order and the victim):

```
d20 + victim's guile modifier + 4 per world it keeps listeners on within range
    + 4 if it has a watcher on the raider's ground
  ≥ DARK_DC (16) + raider's guile modifier
```

At equal guile and no help that is 25% a turn; with a listener in range, 45%.
Those numbers are a starting point to sweep, not a choice.

**What unmasking gives the victim: proof.** Recommended over the brainstorm's
"full cost, doubled, at once":

- The victim's own regard drops by the doubled owed amount immediately — it
  knows now.
- The raid goes open from that turn and is charged like any other.
- The victim is handed a **dossier with a new secret kind, `dark_raid`**
  (subject the raider, ref the order). That is the existing secrets machinery:
  `publish_dossier` charges the raider the doubled owed reputation with every
  onlooker and the doubled owed heat; `blackmail` turns it into a strong hook;
  holding it does nothing.
- The secret stays live while that raider has a raid on the victim, and for
  `DARK_PROOF_TURNS` (6) after its last prize — after that it is old news, like
  any other proof.

This makes the victim decide what being robbed in the dark is worth, and the
bots already decide it well: `useProof` publishes against an enemy and
blackmails anyone else.

### Bots

Whether a raid runs dark is the raider's own call, made per raid. A
model-driven Drajk makes it in its reaction like any other order; its bot weighs
the trade a player would — half the prizes, against the standing and heat an
open raid costs. The bot's exact rule is settled when it is built and measured.

### UI and prompts

- Orders panel: the raider's own raid is marked dark, with the owed tally.
- Rumours: *"unknown raiders at Oridin"*.
- The victim's Trade tab: *"lost to raiders: N"*, attributed or not — which needs
  the ledger fix below.
- `resolution.md`: how to declare a raid dark (*"raid them quietly, no colours"*)
  and that it takes fewer prizes. `appraisal.md`: price it as `guile`.
  `reaction.md`: an NPC robbed in the dark suspects and cannot prove, and does
  not name the raider as fact.

### Prerequisite: serve the true ledger

The victim can only see what it lost if its ledger is the server's. Add the
player's ledger to `CampaignView.effective` beside the stats and span, and have
the Trade tab and the player's Factions row read it. Rivals' rows stay
estimates from the redacted world, which is honest — they are.

### Measurement

The harness, with and without dark raids: Drajk's heat, the Combine's regard
for Drajk, how often a dark raid is unmasked and by whom, how often the proof
is published against blackmailed, and the board at 30 and 100 turns.

### Journal

No old journal can hold a dark raid, and open raids are untouched, so no
version pin — except that anonymising bounty-payout log lines must apply to
dark raids only.

### Open decisions

1. **What unmasking does.** Proof the victim chooses how to use (recommended),
   or the brainstorm's immediate doubled scandal.
2. **The prize penalty.** Decided: `DARK_RAID_YIELD` 0.5. Dark takes half
   the prizes, so it is a real trade against the heat and standing it saves.

**Size: M.**

## 2. Intel with memory — *Stellaris*

*Revised 2026-10-05, against the code after #56 and #57.* The fog now holds:
other powers' paper no longer reaches the payload, the player's ledger is served
true, and a raid can run dark. The first version's open decision 1 is settled by
that, and the design below is written to be built in three slices.

### The problem, in play

Sight is a snapshot. A watcher at Vantic shows the Vigil's capital-ship
programme in full; the turn the watcher is burned, the programme is a rumour
again — *"something under way at Vantic, 2 of 5"* — and the player is asked to
forget what they saw. Everything else about a rival is a rumour of four facts
(whose, where, how long, how far along) however long the player has been
watching them. Spending on spies buys a light that goes out, never knowledge
that builds up.

### What exists today

- **Hidden**, so the only things intel can reveal: orders in the eight secret
  categories (four covert, four yards), unexposed operatives, covert stat
  debuffs, and a rival's secrets.
- **Seen in full** by a watcher at the order's origin or target, by a listener
  within `EMISSION_RANGE` for emitting work, and by presence for physical work.
- **Never named**: a raid run dark. Its owner is a mystery to everyone but the
  raider, watchers included; tracing it is the victim's roll in the tick.
- **Inert**: `counter_intelligence`. It is a duration category with a floor and
  a place in the covert set, and nothing reads it — it completes and changes
  nothing. Intel gives it a job.
- **Already free**, so no threshold can reveal it: fleet totals (exact, from
  public movements) and treasuries.

### The design: two halves

**1. Memory — what you saw stays seen.**

`WorldState.sightings`: one row per (viewer, order) for every order a power has
seen in full that is neither its own nor public. Written in the tick, after
orders advance:

```
{ viewer, orderId, factionId, type, label, originId, targetId,
  progress, durationTurns, delivers, seenTurn }
```

- **Refreshed** every turn the order is still seen in full.
- **Kept** when the order drops back to a rumour, and shown as a last-seen row:
  *"last seen turn 7: capital ship construction at Vantic, 2 of 5 — due by turn
  10 if it ran on."* The projected turn is arithmetic on what was seen.
- **Still live**, when it is: the server knows which rumour is the remembered
  order, so it shows the remembered row marked *still under way* and drops the
  anonymous rumour. You can tell it is still going on; you cannot see how far.
- **Dropped** after `INTEL_MEMORY_TURNS` (8) unseen, or the turn the viewer
  sees it finish. **Never** on a completion the viewer did not see — removing
  the row then would tell them it had completed.
- **No id in the payload**, by the rumour's reasoning: a remembered order cannot
  be handed to `interrupt_order`. The server matches on it; `worldAsSeenBy`
  ships only the viewer's rows, without ids.
- **A dark raid is never remembered**, because it is never seen in full.

**2. Intel — a level per pair, built up and worn down.**

`Faction.intel`: subject → 0..100, beside `disposition`, private to its holder
(`worldAsSeenBy` strips every other power's map).

| grows each turn by | |
|---|---|
| each watcher of yours at work on their ground | +6 |
| each world of yours with listeners within range of their ground | +3 |
| a live `trade_accord` with them | +2 |
| a trade run of yours selling in their markets (once §3 exists) | +2 |
| each battle fought against them | +5 |
| each of their operatives you take | +10, once |
| decay | −2 |
| each `counter_intelligence` programme they run on their own ground | −4 more |

One watcher nets +4 a turn: 20 by turn 5, 40 by 10, 60 by 15, 80 by 20. Two
watchers reach 80 in eight turns. A sweep of their own cuts a lone watcher's
gain to nothing.

| at | what it reveals about that power | read by |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | its rumours carry the order's **type** — *"a capital-ship programme at Vantic"* | display, prompts |
| 40 | its rumours carry **what the work delivers** — hulls by class, a fixture, a developed world | display, prompts |
| 60 | its unexposed operatives **on your ground** show on your System tab, and roll exposure 1 in 20 worse | the reducer |
| 80 | your watchers dig its secrets at +3, and you trace its dark raids at +4 | the reducer |

**The dark-raid rule.** Intel is keyed on a subject, and a dark rumour has none,
so no level names a dark raider. What 80 buys is a better tracing roll: the
victim's code already knows whose raid it is rolling against, so knowing that
power's habits can help the roll without telling the player anything first.

**Counter-intelligence finally does something.** While a `counter_intelligence`
programme runs on a world its holder controls, every other power's intel on that
holder decays 4 more a turn, and rival operatives on that world roll exposure
1 in 20 worse. One reader per effect, both read live off `pendingOrders`, the
way a blockade is.

### Where it touches the rest

- `observeOrders` takes the viewer's intel on each order's owner and returns a
  richer rumour: `OrderRumour` gains optional `type` and `delivers`.
- `worldAsSeenBy` ships the viewer's `remembered` rows (no ids) and only the
  viewer's `intel` map.
- `agentsVisibleTo` adds a rival's operatives on the viewer's worlds at 60+.
- The tick's operative pass reads the 60 and counter-intelligence exposure
  terms; the watcher's dig reads 80; `unmaskRoll` reads 80.
- **Prompts.** Last-seen lines join the orders block, and each faction row in
  an NPC's state block carries its own intel level on that power. A reaction
  call that remembers a rival's yard programme can mention it.
- **UI.** A small intel bar per rival on the Factions panel, the next threshold
  named on hover; last-seen rows greyed in the Orders panel; typed rumours in
  the briefing's *Unidentified activity*.

### Bots, and why the harness can see half of it

The bots read no rival orders and stay that way, so the display thresholds (20,
40) are invisible to them. The mechanical thresholds are not: the bots already
run watchers and saboteurs and catch each other's operatives, so 60 and 80 show
up as operatives caught and proofs filed, both of which the harness counts.

One new rule, in `proposeFor` beside the covert ones: **a bot that has caught a
rival's operative on its ground in the last five turns runs a
counter-intelligence sweep at its best world**, one at a time. It cannot see
anybody's intel on it, so it reacts to what it can see: being spied on. Without
the rule the counter is a mechanic nobody uses.

### Measurement

- Levels each pair reaches over 30 and 100 turns; every threshold reached by
  someone, or it is retuned.
- Operatives caught and secrets filed, with intel on and off. The board should
  barely move; if it moves a lot, 60 is too strong.
- `pnpm perf`: the tick now runs `observeOrders` for every power every turn.

### Journal

New state and new tick work that changes outcomes (exposure, digging, tracing),
so `JOURNAL_VERSION` 15 with `LegacyRules.intel`. An older journal replays with
no intel, no memory and an inert counter-intelligence.

### Build order

1. **Memory.** Sightings, the last-seen rows, the merged rumour, the prompts.
   Valuable alone, and nothing else depends on it.
2. **Levels and the display thresholds**: accrual, decay, typed rumours, the
   Factions bar.
3. **The mechanical thresholds and counter-intelligence**, with the bot rule,
   measured.

### Decisions

1. **Memory for every power** (recommended: the NPC prompts get it for free) or
   the player only.
2. **The four thresholds above**, or a different ladder. The one most worth a
   second look is 60: seeing a rival's operatives on your own ground is the
   strongest thing on the list.
3. **Counter-intelligence as the counter** (recommended: it is a category that
   already exists and does nothing) or a new op.
4. **Intel private** (recommended) or public, so a power knows it is being
   watched closely. Private keeps the counter a judgement call.

**Size: M+**, three slices of about S each.

## 3. Speculative trade — *Traveller*

*Revised 2026-10-05, against the code after #56 and #57.* Running dark exists
now, so a raid on a convoy can be unowned, and the pricing below has been sized
rather than guessed.

### The problem, in play

Money comes from what you hold: worlds pay, hubs pay, lanes pay whoever sits on
them. A power with ships and no ground earns only by contesting someone else's,
and a freighter earns only on lanes that cross unaligned ground. There is no way
to make money by *moving things* — no reason to care that an industrial moon is
three jumps from a world that needs what it makes — and so no convoys for a
raider to hunt or an escort to guard.

### What exists today

- **Freighters**: Meridian 6, the Combine 4, Drajk 1 on the opening board,
  weighted ×2 on unaligned hops (`FREIGHTER_LANE_WEIGHT`) and idle everywhere
  else.
- **`worldType`**: seven kinds, three or four worlds each. It decides a stat
  bonus and the fixtures a world takes, and nothing about trade. Three of the
  four ice worlds are unaligned.
- **Two goods systems already**: each power's commodity (made at 2 a turn, worth
  10 to anyone else — a gift economy for peace) and the "stuff" assets (ore,
  salvage, contraband, relic), which the Rim's shortages raise ×1.5. Trade must
  not become a third that competes with them; see "Not assets" below.
- **Raiding** diverts lane income on the hops a lane crosses and at either end;
  `trade_accord` and `protection` make a power immune; a letter of marque
  licenses it; a raid can run dark at half yield.
- The brainstorm called this *"micro-scale for a grand-strategy turn"*. The
  answer is to declare a route once, not a shipment every turn.

### The design

**A standing run.** One declaration — *"run tooling from Corvid to Gorrun Deep"*
— sets up a `trade_run`: freighters (and any escorts sent with them) sail
there, sell, buy that world's goods, sail back, sell those, and go again, until
recalled. The player chooses two worlds; their types decide the rest.

**What each kind of world makes and wants**, one of each, a closed table so
every good has a buyer:

| world type | makes | wants |
|---|---|---|
| industrialmoon | tooling | grain |
| gasgiant | fuel | tooling |
| oceanic | grain | fuel |
| arid | ore | water |
| ice | water | luxuries |
| earthlike | luxuries | ore |
| earthnight | data | luxuries |

`ore` is the existing stuff kind on purpose, so a Rim shortage of ore reaches
the trade price with no second mechanism. The other six join the shortage
event's candidates whenever a run is carrying them.

**Price**, per unit, derived and never stored:

```
TRADE_BASE (10)
  × MAKES 0.6 where it is made · WANTS 1.4 where it is wanted · 1.0 elsewhere
  × shortageFactor
  × (1 + pressure[world][good])
```

**Pressure is the one new state, and it is the bound.** `WorldState.markets`,
sparse, `pressure` from −0.5 to +0.5. Buying a unit pushes it up
`MARKET_STEP` (0.01); selling pushes it down; it recovers toward zero
`MARKET_RECOVERY` (0.05) a turn. A lot trades at the price halfway through its
own impact, so splitting a lot buys nothing.

**That is what bounds it, and it can be stated as a number.** A market recovers
0.30 in the six turns of a three-jump round trip, which is 30 units at 0.01 a
unit — five freighters. Five on that route trade at the same prices forever; a
sixth pushes pressure a little higher every trip until the margin is gone.
Trade therefore spreads over the map, a few freighters to a route, instead of
becoming one engine that a rich power scales without limit.

**Sized against development, not guessed.** Those five freighters (30 units)
from Corvid, an industrial moon, to Gorrun Deep, a gas giant three jumps away,
buy tooling at 6.9 and sell it at 11.9: 150 a delivery. The backhaul, fuel
sold at Corvid at the plain price, adds 48. That is 33 a turn against 15 of
upkeep, so 225 credits of freighters pay back in about twelve turns — the
payback `developmentCost` charges, with raids and tariffs still to come off.

**Where the money goes**, each piece a rule the economy already has:

- **Buying pays the source world's holder** — a transfer, which is why a power
  wants traders at its worlds. Buying at your own or an unaligned world pays
  nobody.
- **Selling is paid by the market, so the margin is minted.** Bounded by
  pressure, the way commodities are bounded by production.
- **Tariffs follow toll policy.** If the selling world's holder tolls the
  trader, it takes `BASE_TOLL_RATE` (12%) of the sale; an extortionist takes
  `TOLL_RATE`. Each hop on the path held by a power that tolls the trader takes
  its rate of the cargo's buying value. No new policy: waiving a toll now waives
  it for convoys too, which makes lifting one worth more across a table.

**The order.** `trade_run` is a new order type and duration category: a movement
(duration computed from the path, never estimated), public like a fleet
movement, carrying only freighters and escorts. It arrives as a guest — contests
no income, fights no battle — and on arrival settles, reverses its path, resets
its progress and goes again under the same id. Recalled with `cancel_order`, it
stops at the world it is at. Declared like any action; the arbiter prices it on
`industry`, and a failed check sends the run with no first cargo.

**Risk on the road**, all from mechanics that exist:

- **Raids.** A run passing through a system a raid is working loses
  `RAID_SHARE` × `raidYield` of its cargo's buying value to the raider —
  unless the run's escort tonnage matches the raider's force at the raid's
  origin. That is escort duty, the job escorts have never had in peacetime.
  Loot is heat and reputation like any prize, licensed by a marque, half and
  unnamed when the raid runs dark, and drawn against a bounty on the trader.
- **Immunity** as for lanes: a `trade_accord` or `protection` with the raider.
- **Blockades and storms** halt the run at the previous world, as they halt a
  movement. A smuggler runs both.
- **War.** A run cannot be sent to a power the trader is at war with. If war
  breaks out under way, the holder impounds the cargo on arrival (it takes the
  buying value) and the convoy turns back empty.

**Ethics reach it through the hooks they already have**: the smuggler runs
blockades and storms, the extortionist's tariff is its toll rate, the autarkist
keeps `AUTARKIC_ROUTE_FRACTION` of a run's margin, the free trader's openness
bonus is not applied (it is about lanes). No new ethic rules.

**Not assets.** Cargo is carried by the order and settled in credits; it is
never held, traded or ransomed. Commodities stay the gift economy between
powers, stuff stays the prizes attempts produce, and trade is the market. True
speculation — buy into a glut, warehouse, sell into a shortage — would need
cargo as assets at a world plus buy and sell ops, and is left for later.

### Ledger and UI

- **Sales are lump sums**, like hull bounties, so not a ledger rate. Each
  arrival is a briefing line (*"6 freighters sold 36 tooling at Gorrun Deep for
  443; bought 36 fuel"*), and `Ledger.trade` reports a trailing average so the
  Trade tab and the counsellor can see what routes are worth.
- **System tab**: what the world makes and wants and what it pays now, beside
  the fixture list.
- **Trade tab**: runs under way with cargo, ETA and last margin; a market table.
- **Map**: runs drawn as fleets are, in the trader's colour, dashed.

### Bots

- **Meridian** (free trader, six freighters) runs one route at a time from its
  own worlds to the best-margin market within four jumps it is not at war with,
  preferring friends' worlds, and buys freighters for it while the route pays.
- **The Combine** (four freighters) runs one where the margin is obvious.
- **Escorts** go with a run whose path crosses a world a raider has worked in
  the last five turns.
- **Drajk** weighs a convoy's cargo into where it raids.

Without bot runs the harness cannot see any of this, and without the Drajk rule
the convoy risk is never measured.

### Measurement

- **Trade income as its own figure.** `tests/balance.test.ts` bounds the lane
  share of income at 20–50%; trade must not quietly enter that bound.
- **Sweep** `TRADE_BASE`, `MARKET_STEP`, `MARKET_RECOVERY` and
  `CARGO_PER_FREIGHTER` over 30 and 100 turns: what Meridian earns, whether the
  board moves, how often convoys are raided, and whether escorts change that.
- **The saturation claim**: a second and third six freighters on one route earn
  less. A test pins it.

### Journal

No old journal holds a run, and markets default to empty, so no pin. The bot
rules change bot ops, which are journaled, so saves replay as they were played.

### Build order

1. **The table and prices, read-only**: goods, `markets`, prices on the System
   and Trade tabs. Lets the map be read for trade before anything moves.
2. **The run**: the order type, movement, settlement, the loop, recall, the
   briefing lines and the trailing ledger figure.
3. **The road**: raids and escorts, tariffs and tolls, blockades, storms and war.
4. **The bots and the sweep.**

### Decisions

1. **A standing run** (recommended) or one-shot shipments.
2. **Cargo carried by the order** (recommended) or warehoused as assets.
3. **The sale mints, bounded by pressure** (recommended) or the selling world's
   holder pays, which conserves money but makes every delivery need its consent.
4. **Escorts protect a convoy** (recommended) or raids always take their share.
5. **Goods names**: tooling, fuel, grain, ore, water, luxuries, data — or names
   in the setting's own voice.

**Size: L**, four slices. Larger than the first estimate: a new movement order
that loops, a market, and risk on the road.

## Suggested order

1. ~~**Running dark**, with the served-ledger fix.~~ Built (#56).
2. ~~**Fix the fog leaks**.~~ Built (#56).
3. **Intel with memory**, memory first: it stands alone and is the most visible
   change for the smallest build.
4. **Speculative trade**, last: largest, and it is better with intel built,
   since a trade run into a power's markets is one of intel's sources and a
   convoy is something a watcher would want to see coming.
