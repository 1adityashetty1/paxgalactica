# Playtest 2026-09-09 — Meridian, ten turns to the epilogue (`playtest_0909`)

Player: **Meridian Trade Authority** (`meridian`), title **Chief Executive**.
influence 17 / industry 16 / guile 13 / might 10 / resolve 9. Credits 2400.
Systems sek-1, sek-2, sek-4, tor-1. `maxTurns: 10`, so the ending ran too.

Brief, in three named categories rather than an open hunt:

- **A** — features built since the last playtest that are broken;
- **B** — actions accepted but not manifested in world state;
- **C** — performance.

**The first playtest to see any of it.** Nothing built since 2026-09-07 had been
exercised by a live model: no persona had seen an asset, a fixture, a per-tick
yield, a contingency, a `contract` treaty, a hired squadron or the advisor in a
prompt, and `channelBlockers` had never been observed firing.

## How it was run, and why that matters

Two `adversarial-player` agents were started and **both stalled** — their own
stream watchdogs, not the server, which stayed responsive at ~2ms throughout.
The campaign was finished by a scripted driver: a list of actions, a `curl` per
step, `GET /api/campaign` captured before and after each one, and the pair
diffed by script rather than read by eye.

That is worth recording because it is item **95**'s harness in miniature, it
worked without incident, and **category B is only answerable that way** — an
inert success looks exactly like a real one in the narrative, and the diff is
the only thing that can tell them apart.

## A — broken

Every finding here returned **HTTP 200 and a plausible narrative**. Nothing
threw. That is the category's whole character.

### A1 — `looksLikeAPlan` misses an enumerated plan · FIXED

Turn 4, verbatim: *"Three things, Chief Executive, and then I'll stop. **First**
— Shalka… **Second**, the Combine ledger generally… **Third** — the Drajk."*

Both branches returned false. The line branch found no line beginning with a
marker, because the counsel is one unbroken paragraph. The prose branch was
`\bfirst\b[^.]*\.[^.]*\bsecond\b` — exactly one full stop between markers — and
real counsel puts several sentences under each heading.

Fixed by counting ordinals followed by punctuation. A heading writes `First —`
or `Second,`; ordinary speech writes "the first time" or "First Elder" with a
word next. Two required, for the same reason two line markers are.

### A2 — a contingency pays out on a voluntary sale · FIXED

Meridian sold `tor-1` to the Vigil under a `cession` for 250 credits. The
`war_indemnity` contingency on `com-0-1`, trigger `world_lost` by meridian at
`tor-1`, has `firedTurn: 3` and paid **300**. Net **+550 for handing over a
world**, and the insurance was collected on the seller's own decision.

`voidConditionMet` is a pure state predicate — *does this power still hold it* —
so it could not tell a conquest from a signature, and the tick cedes territory
immediately before it settles contingencies. A live `cession` naming the world
is now proof it changed hands by agreement: derived rather than recorded, so no
per-turn set and no schema change, and it holds whether the handover ran at
signature or at ratification.

Replaying the campaign under the fix now shows Meridian 2993cr against the live
save's 3293 — exactly the indemnity no longer paid.

### A3 — `/endtalk` could destroy a finished negotiation · FIXED

Turn 2, `drajk`, **HTTP 502**:

```
the arbiter considers it: output failed validation after 3 attempts.
  - (root): Invalid input: expected object, received string
```

The accord appraisal exhausted its retries on a malformed arbiter response, the
error propagated out of `closeChannel`, and `endTalk` had **already cleared** the
channel, the history and the concessions before awaiting it. No ops, no
transcript, no channel to retry. A completed negotiation deleted by a model
glitch that had nothing to do with it.

Two layers now. A ruling that cannot be obtained returns nothing and says so in
the notes rather than throwing — safe because it is not the only guard, since
`sendMessage` appraises every player concession as it is made and those blockers
refuse the accord downstream. And any other failure restores the conversation
exactly as it stood, so `/endtalk` can simply be tried again.

### A4 — the principle matcher cannot read a paraphrase · FIXED

Turn 8. The sheet says *"will not **repudiate** a **contract** it has signed"*;
the ruling came back *"forbids **repudiating contracts** it has signed"*. Exact
token equality scored that at **0.44** against a threshold of 0.6, so the most
flagrant red-line breach of the campaign matched nothing and exited through
`admissible: false` — the one ruling that charges nothing at all.

Fixed with a six-character inflection prefix and a **swept** threshold: the
missed case now scores 0.556, and the worst overlap between any two *different*
factions' lines is 0.286 — the number that must stay below it, since matching a
rival's line would price an act against a principle its power does not hold. 0.5
sits in that gap with room on both sides.

The same ruling also **fabricated its facts** — *"bound by a five-turn charter
signed this turn (turn 8, contract term until turn 13)"* — when the loan had been
established on turn 3 and had already returned. The classification is fixed; the
invention is not, and nothing currently checks it.

### A5 — the fixture half of the asset system is unreachable · FIXED (prompt)

Asked twice for a works — an exchange on turn 2, a mine on turn 5 — the model
wrote `establish_commitment` with `incomePerTurn` both times. Nothing errors and
the income is real, so `portable: false` and `yield`, built specifically for
mines, exchanges and theatres, were never reached.

`resolution.md` now says which mechanism owns a works, and why: written as a
commitment a mine can never be raided, conquered, sold or lost.

### A6 — withdrawn

`channelBlockers` stayed `[]` while the player proposed closing a lane, against
Meridian's verbatim red line. **Not a defect.** The blocker fires on *recorded
concessions* and the Free Worlds declined, so there was none. All three paths
were walked before closing it — see item 96 in `todo.md`.

## B — accepted, not manifested

### B1 — 90 credits paid for an asset that never moved · FIXED

The recorded concession at close:

```jsonc
{ "by": "ojjul", "kind": "asset_sale", "assets": ["ast-0-0"],
  "text": "Sell the Ithaal outer-strata assay outright to Meridian for 90 credits." }
```

Extraction emitted `adjust_credits -90` / `+90`, two dispositions, a
`log_narrative` — and **no `transfer_asset`**. Zero rejections. Credits moved.
`ast-0-0` is still `heldBy: "ojjul"` in the final state.

The narrative papered over it: *"paid in full this turn against the file already
sitting on Meridian's ledger"* — inventing prior possession to explain the
missing half.

**Not a guard that was slipped past; there was no guard.** A world sale is one
object — a `cession` carrying `terms.territory` and `terms.payment`, settled by
two calls that sit together at both sites precisely so the halves cannot come
apart. An asset sale had no object at all: a loose transfer beside a loose
payment with nothing binding them.

`terms.assets` is the fix, and it is the same argument that produced
`terms.payment` one layer down — a one-time *price* had no home and a world cost
240 credits; the one-time *consideration* had no home either.

### B2 — a loan record that contradicts its own stack

`loan-3-0.text` reads *"Ten Combine hulls sail under Meridian colours"*;
`lent.stack` is `{ battleship: 4 }`. The reducer trimmed correctly to what the
lender actually held at that world — but the stored text is what reaches the
player and what gets replayed into the personas. **Open.**

Nothing else diffed empty. `t5-mine` (a partial) staged its commitment and its
−210 charge and both landed; `t7-consume` took the contraband 20 → 10 crates
exactly as narrated; `t4-sedition` placed an operative.

## C — performance

| call | n | min | median | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| action | 14 | 8.6s | **42.9s** | 117.4s |
| advisor | 3 | 17.6s | 22.9s | 31.3s |
| talk | 15 | 11.4s | 26.9s | 80.8s |
| endtalk | 7 | 34.2s | 58.9s | 82.1s |
| endturn | 9 | — | **62.4s** | 112.8s |

**It is not a slowdown.** The end-of-turn sequence across ten turns was 63, 67,
80, 62, 113, 50, 33 — no trend. `GET /api/campaign` stayed flat at **1.7–2.2ms**
with the payload at 92KB against 43KB at turn 0 and the log at 178 entries, so
the `eventLogFrom`/`spliceLog` work is holding. The three 0.02s end-of-turns are
correct: nothing was staged, so reactions were skipped.

What it is instead is a flat ~150s a turn, every turn — and CLAUDE.md's own
table (~33s an action, ~38s an end of turn) understates it.

**Cost tracks wall clock almost exactly** across all 24 calls, which says the
time is output-token generation rather than overhead. The two exceptions are the
finding:

- one action ran **923.93s** and returned **HTTP 000** — no response at all;
- one ran 117s while costing $0.10, a tenth of the money for four times the time.

Neither was generation. Both killed a playtest agent. `query()` has no timeout of
its own, so a wedged child process is waited on forever; there is now a
**per-message deadline** of 180s — six times the slowest legitimate call — after
which the attempt is abandoned and retried like any other transient failure.

**What was measured after the fact, from inside the process:** appraisal 12–14.7s
for two numbers and a clause with thinking already disabled, resolution 19–24s,
reaction 29–31s each. The appraisal figure is the interesting one: it is almost
entirely transport — binary spawn plus the SDK's carrier round trip, which
CLAUDE.md already prices at ~7s for a trivial call. That is the floor, and item
**95** is the only thing that moves it.

Reactions were split one-per-faction as a **correctness** change (no power reads
another's observation block; a misattributed reaction becomes detectable; one bad
response costs one voice). In parallel it also took end-of-turn to a 51.0s
median, and the parallelism was **given back**: N calls each re-send
`serializeState` and the reaction prompt for 2.8x the cost, and concurrency
spawns N binaries which contend.

## What this run did not settle

- **`return_loan` and `repudiate_loan` were never exercised.** The automatic
  return at `dueTurn` beat both attempts, so the two deliberate ops remain
  untested outside the suite.
- **No fixture was ever created**, so the fixture-transfer refusal is tested and
  never seen.
- **No `dossier` was created inside an accord.**
- Item **92**'s two original claims — marriages and ceremonial arrangements
  across disposition, and whether an NPC ever chooses to suborn Meridian at
  resolve 9 — were not touched.
