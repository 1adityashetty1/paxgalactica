# Audit of the 2026-10-18 playtest

Every finding in `docs/playtest-2026-10-18.md` was checked against the code,
the journal of `saves/pt-session-1018.json` (which replays byte-identically)
and the raw responses in `saves/pt18-out-*.json`. Each one was then filed as:

- **band-aid** — a local fault with a local fix. Fixed on `claude/intent-ops`
  and pinned by `tests/playtest-1018.test.ts`, which shows each reducer rule
  both ways.
- **architectural** — a symptom of how responsibility is divided. Patching
  the instance leaves the class open. Reported below, not fixed.
- **by design**, **not reproducible** or **wrong** — no fix.

Reducer changes are pinned to `JOURNAL_VERSION` 26, one `LegacyRules` flag
each. Every save replays identically under the new code, the playtest's own
included. The four harness boards are unchanged: 5/5/6/5/4 at 30 and 100 turns
with events and at 30 without, and 5/6/6/5/3 at 100 without. The suite has
2,230 tests, all passing.

## Architectural findings

### 1. Acts the code detects never reach the principle ruling (B4, C10, C11)

The arbiter rules on breaches from the declaration's prose, before the roll. The
code learns for certain that an act breaks a treaty only afterwards, when
resolution writes `breaks: tre-0-3` or the reducer charges `attack_under_pact`.
Nothing then asks whether the act crossed a line.

- **B4.** Meridian's "will not repudiate a contract it has signed" refused
  "repudiate the pact, then take Kalzir" at +8 dissent. "Sail to take Kalzir"
  carried `breaks: tre-0-3`, broke the pact, and never met the line.
- **C10, C11.** The counterparty's lines are worse off. In accords they are
  priced only when the arbiter happens to name one from concession prose, so
  Drajk ("will not put its name to a written treaty") signed a public
  `contract` uncharged.

**Why a band-aid won't hold:** whether a breach is spotted is judgement, and
CLAUDE.md already records that it is unstable. Re-asking the arbiter when
`breaks` appears is one more call that can miss the same way.

**Shape of the fix:** a line names the acts it forbids. `acts.ts` already
enumerates breach kinds, and `Compulsion.barsPeaceWith` already turns one line
into ids at seed time. Tag each red line and compulsion with the
`BreachKind`s or op kinds it covers. For example, Meridian's contract line
covers `treaty_repudiated`, `attack_under_pact` and `loan_defaulted`; Drajk's
written-treaty line covers `form_treaty`. Then check every mechanically
detected act against the actor's tagged lines after staging, on the declared,
reaction and accord paths alike. Judgement stays with the arbiter for acts the
code cannot detect. **Touches:** the seed sheets, `acts.ts`, `turn.ts` (the
declared path and `closeChannel`), and the reaction path. Old journals replay
under a flag.

### 2. A bargain is not a transaction (C4's class; B9 of 2026-10-15)

An accord is extracted as a flat list of ops. When the reducer rejects one,
the correction call writes a smaller list, and the consideration lands without
what it paid for. Meridian's 400 for a toll lift landed while the lift was refused,
in two separate channels. The 2026-10-15 playtest found the same shape
in a marriage paid for with a toll lift. C4 is fixed, but the next leg the
reducer refuses will half-land the same way.

**Shape of the fix:** an accord is a set of bargains, each a concession and
its consideration. Extraction groups ops by the concession they enact; the
concession record already carries the ids. A correction may repair a leg and
may not drop one: if a leg cannot land, its whole bargain goes, consideration
included, and the transcript says so. **Touches:** the extraction schema and
prompt, `stageWithCorrection` on the accord path, and `groundInConcessions`.

### 3. Extraction re-derives what the concession record already holds (C7, C8)

Each persona records what it gives up, resolved to ids: worlds, credits, a
per-turn flow, hulls, assets and favours. Extraction then reads the transcript
and writes the ops again from prose, and `groundInConcessions` checks them
against the record. The band-aid shows extraction the record. It still writes
the ops, and can still mint a dossier where the record names `ast-0-1`.

**Shape of the fix:** the engine writes the concrete transfers from the
record:

- a world: a `cession`'s territory;
- credits: `pay`;
- a thing: `transfer_asset`;
- a favour: `release_obligation` or `assign_obligation`;
- a toll lift: `set_toll_policy`.

Extraction is left only what has no structured field: treaty types and terms,
commitments, void conditions. Grounding becomes construction, and a dropped or
duplicated transfer stops being possible. **Touches:** the extraction prompt,
`closeChannel`, and the concession schema (a toll lift needs a field).

### 4. Resolution may write ops the ruling never named (D3; and the B7 class)

On a critical success, resolution is told to add "one concrete bonus", and it
holds the whole `ModelOpSchema`. Four filters close one op each:

- sends (`sendsAsRuled`);
- prizes (`prizesAsRuled`);
- sways (`swaysAsRolled`);
- commitments (`commitmentsAsRuled`).

D3 closed fleets. An `interrupt_order` on a rival's programme, an
`extend_order`, or a `cancel_order` reaching past presence are still a
flourish away.

**Shape of the fix:** the ruling's overt part names the op families its act
may produce. The crit bonus becomes a closed menu priced in code: a cost
avoided, a sway, a named prize. `opsAsRuled` then replaces the per-op filters.
**Touches:** `DeclaredRulingSchema`, `appraisal.md`, `resolution.md`, and
`refusals.ts`.

### 5. The story is written before the verdict (D4; C13's epilogue slips)

Resolution writes narrative and ops in one call, and the reducer's rejections
and corrections arrive afterwards as notes. The band-aid writes the correction
onto the batch. Everything downstream still reads the first story wherever a
note does not reach: the persona transcripts, the epilogue's source, and the
reactions' prose.

**Shape of the fix:** narrate what landed. One option is a narration pass
after `applyOps`, from the applied ops and the notes. A cheaper option is a
reconcile pass that runs only when something was rejected or trimmed. Either
adds a call, so it is a cost decision. **Touches:** `resolveAction`,
`stageWithCorrection`, and the reaction brief.

### 6. A prize's worth is the model's (B7)

`yields` fixes the kind of prize before the roll. Its quantity and
`valuePerUnit` are still resolution's choice: helium-3 came back as 15 tons at
8, then 12 at 12, sold for 120 and 130. `ASSET_ARCHETYPES` sets defaults only
for the kinds it knows.

**Shape of the fix:** quantity from the check's margin and the world's
income, worth from the archetype table or a per-kind rule. **Touches:**
`assets.ts`, the `create_asset` handling in the reducer, and `resolution.md`.

### 7. One-party income commitments mint money (B5)

A declared charter ruled `income` pays its own power up to 25 a turn, 50 in
total for Meridian, with no source, for one action each. That is inside
`MAX_COMMITMENT_INCOME` and `maxCommitmentIncomeFor` by design, but it is the
one remaining place where a ruling, rather than a mechanism, creates a
standing income.

**Decision needed:** give a charter's income a source (a route share, or a
fixture's yield), or accept it as priced.

### 8. Accords still move regard for nothing (C9)

Since intent-ops §3 a declaration's regard is rolled and a reaction's is
priced, but an accord still narrates `adjust_disposition`. That is held to 25
per pair per batch, and a treaty pays its own goodwill. Channels are
unmetered, so warmth costs one conversation: +2/+1 from flattery, +8/+8 from a
sale.

**Decision needed:** price accord warmth from the `REGARD_BECAUSE` table
(`promise_kept`, `deference`), or leave it to treaty goodwill alone.

## Limits of the band-aids

- **B2** blocks any instant move out of a world that received one in the same
  batch, including hulls that were standing there already: stacks carry no
  hull identity. Two declarations in one turn can still make two instant
  jumps, which the design accepts.
- **C7, C8** depend on extraction reading the record it is now shown.
  Finding 3 is the fix that does not.
- **D3** closes fleets only. Finding 4 covers the rest.
