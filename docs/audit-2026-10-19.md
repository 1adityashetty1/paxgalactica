# Audit of the 2026-10-19 playtest (following the advisor)

`docs/playtest-2026-10-19-advisor.md`, checked against the code and the
campaign's journal (`saves/advisor_follow.json`). Each finding was reproduced
before it was classed:

- a **band-aid** is a local fix that closes the bug where it happens, and it is
  made;
- a **structural** problem recurs because of how the system is shaped, and it is
  written up below and left open.

Band-aids are pinned to journal **version 29** where a reducer rule changed.
Tests: `tests/playtest-1019.test.ts`, plus one updated case in
`tests/reconcile.test.ts`. After the fixes:

- every save replays identically, and the new playtest save replays with no
  rejection;
- the four harness boards do not move;
- 2,289 tests pass.

### Prompt band-aids

These are weaker than code, and are listed as such.

- **`diplomacy-persona.md`**: strike the other side's withdrawals with a
  retraction under **their** `by` (B4).
- **`extraction.md`**: a withdrawn term is not accepted, even if it is still on
  the record (B4).
- **`advisor.md`**: lead with the point that matters most, under 500
  characters (A1). Check whose garrison, fleet or world it is before naming it
  (A3, the garrison misattribution).
- **Pronouns, in all four prompts that write people** (resolution, advisor,
  reaction, persona): officers, operatives and notables are they/them; a leader
  goes by the sheet's pronouns, or by title when the sheet is not shown.

## Structural problems, built (second commit)

Each is closed in code, tested in `tests/structural-1019.test.ts`. Every save
replays identically and the four boards do not move.

- **S1:** a `record` commitment beside an act that already has a mechanism
  (work, a move, a bounty, an ultimatum, a payment, an operative) is dropped by
  `commitmentsAsRuled`. A one-party record is shown to every reader as *"a
  statement of policy, owed to nobody — changing course on it breaks no
  promise"*.
- **S2:** `POST /api/talk/:id` takes `strike`, kinds of the player's own
  concessions. They come off the record in code before the persona answers,
  and the message says so. The channel panel has a **withdraw** button on each
  of the player's terms, and `pt.mjs talk … --strike` does the same.
- **S3:** what the reactions are told about a fleet sent at another power's
  world, or into an occupied orbit, is what sailed, *"the outcome is decided
  when they arrive"*, never the story's version of the battle. `resolution.md`:
  narrate the departure, never the battle.
- **S4:** a fleet with no lift arriving over unaligned ground, where only powers
  it is at peace with stand, takes station beside them
  (`BattleRules.stationBesidePeers`, journal version 29).
- **S5:** the counsellor gets its power's voice without the sentence telling it
  to number its points (`voiceWithoutNumbering`).
- **S6:** `pressuresOn` (`src/domain/pressures.ts`) computes what the
  counsellor must weigh: rivals in the orbits of worlds the power holds, with
  the garrison named as its own and what the world still pays; lift massed a
  jump from a garrison it outnumbers; a hostile battle line outweighing the
  local one. It reaches the counsellor as *"what your staff has flagged"*. On
  the playtest's turn-7 board it names the Vigil's 66 troops in lift beside
  Torrek's garrison of 21.

None of these was verified live; the next loop's playtest is that check.

## Structural problems, as found

### S1. The arbiter's `establishes` is unbounded, and a record commitment is mistaken for an obligation (B5)

**What happened.** Three ordinary acts minted commitments:

- `torrek_fortification`, on a fortify;
- `torrek_supremacy`, on a fleet movement, stating as fact *"our fleets hold the
  orbit unchallenged"* before a battle that went the other way;
- `vigil_bounty_expansion`, on a bounty that never landed.

**Why it is structural.** The guard is a prompt sentence, *"programmes, attacks,
ultimatums and tolls are not commitments"*, added after the last playtest and
ignored three times in ten turns. And every reader treats a `record`
commitment as a promise:

- **the accord appraisal** ruled ceding Torrek a repudiation of
  `torrek_fortification`. That crossed a red line and charged +8 dissent on an
  accord already withdrawn;
- **the counsellor** called `torrek_supremacy` "a lying ledger" and spent the
  player's turn-7 action on dissolving it.

A record commitment has three jobs at once: exclusivity, memory for the
arbiter, and a promise. Nothing says which one a given record is.

**Direction.** Code decides when `establishes` is legal, the way §4 decided
reaches:

- refuse it when the overt part's ops already express the act (work, a move, a
  bounty);
- or give a `record` commitment a `kind` of *declaration* rather than
  *undertaking*, which no breach can be measured against.

### S2. The record of a negotiation is written by the other party (B4)

**What happened.** The player said "we withdraw the cession" twice, and it stayed
on the record: retractions are written by the persona, and the persona strikes
only what it chooses to.

**Why it is structural.** §2–3 made the record the deal, and the player's half
of the record is authored by the NPC's model. The band-aid above asks the
persona to strike the player's withdrawals. A prompt can fail that, and when it
does, the close holds the player to a term they withdrew.

**Direction.** Let the player's side carry its own edits:

- a **strike** control on each of the player's concessions in the channel panel,
  applied by `mergeConcessions` as a retraction by the player;
- or a deterministic reading of the player's message for withdrawals of terms
  already on the record.

### S3. A narrative is written before the arrivals it describes, and reactions read it as fact (D2)

**What happened.** On turn 5 the story said "a clean sweep", and on turn 9 "a
rout". Both battles were lost. The Vigil's turn-9 reaction answered the rout,
and the turn-5 `torrek_supremacy` record was minted from the same premature
story.

**Why it is structural.** Resolution narrates at declaration time, reactions run
at end of turn before the tick, and battles settle in the tick. The §5
reconcile pass covers what the reducer did at staging, and arrivals are not
known then.

**Direction:**

- resolution narrates a movement's **departure** and leaves the arrival to the
  battle report: a rule that is checkable, since its ops are a `move`;
- reactions read the staged batch's ops summary for movements, not its prose;
- arguably, reactions run after the tick's arrivals.

### S4. Standing guard over a world someone else guards is an attack (D1)

**What happened.** "Protect Sennex, not take it" was resolved as an assault on
Arkane, a power on good terms with us: 3 ships lost each side, and Sennex −15
toward us.

**Why it is structural.** An arrival at a world with another power present
resolves under one rule: *"anyone already present who is not attacking
defends"*. The only exception is a treaty guest. There is no intent "share the
orbit" for the arbiter to rule or the reducer to honour, and the arbiter does
not warn that the move is a fight.

**Direction:**

- a `move` names its stance on arrival (`station` or `attack`); a stationed
  fleet meeting a non-hostile power's ships coexists in the orbit and contests
  income only;
- or the arbiter rules a guard over a third party's orbit as a negotiation
  (*"that needs Arkane's agreement"*).

### S5. Meridian's voice and the counsellor's plan guard contradict each other (A2)

**What happened.** Six of ten advisor calls failed `looksLikeAPlan` on the first
attempt. On turn 8 the counsel that passed opened "Three things. First…" and
stopped.

**Why it is structural.** One `voice` field serves two contracts. The persona
should number its points; the counsellor must not enumerate. `advisor.md`
already says to ignore the voice here, and the model follows the voice. Each
miss costs a retry, about $0.03.

**Direction.** Give each sheet a separate counsellor voice: `serializeCharacter`
gains a mode that leaves out syntax rules the advisor's contract forbids. Or
the guard lets a single ordinal in running prose pass.

### S6. The counsellor reads the board badly and seldom advises (A3, A4)

**What happened:**

- it invented a position at Neth;
- it called our garrison the Vigil's (now a prompt rule);
- it never mentioned the eleven Vigil lifters massing at Vantic, the most urgent
  fact in turns 7–10;
- three of ten counsels named a problem and no move.

A player who follows it fought the same war three times, because nothing in the
counsel weighed the last attempt's cost before the next.

**Why it is structural.** The counsellor reads the same `serializeState` block
every prompt gets, a flat document in which a massing fleet is one stack among
twenty. Its contract forbids a plan, so "what to do" is left implicit.

**Direction.** Code computes the handful of pressures before the call, as
`concessionBudget` does for a persona:

- threats massing within reach;
- worlds contested and what they cost;
- the last action's outcome.

The counsellor's job is then to choose among them and speak.

## Not acted on

- **Moves between one's own worlds were rolled** (might, DC 5) rather than priced
  trivial. The prompt already says trivial, and a natural 1 is the only risk. If
  it recurs, code should skip the roll when every overt op is an own-ground
  move.
- **The first resolution call cost $0.31**, before the cache was warm. Later
  calls cost about $0.10. This is not a bug.

Every save replays identically, the four boards do not move, and 2,300 tests

parallel run, and was given 20s.

### Structural, open

**L5. The story is written before the engine knows timing.** These are not
battles; §S3's rule covers those. Four examples:

- a two-jump move reinforcing our own world "already in orbit";
- a fortification's ceiling "rises" three turns before it does;
- an open build that lands at once "will take time";
- battleships called "merchant shipping".

Resolution writes the story and the ops in one call, and only the reducer knows
when each op lands. **Direction:** resolution is handed each op's computed
timing (lands now, or in N turns) in a second, cheap pass, or the reconcile
pass runs on every declaration whose ops include an order.

**L8. The counsellor still often describes without advising.** On turns 6 and 8
it restated the threat and offered no move, even when asked for "the one
thing". Its contract forbids a plan, so a direction is left implicit.
**Direction:** a single `recommendation` sentence the guard allows: one act,
never a sequence.

**L9. Following the counsellor leads into battles of mutual ruin.**

- **Loop 1, turn 9:** 21 lost against 19.
- **Loop 2, turn 9:** 17 lost against 18.

The counsel weighs threats, now computed (S6), and never the odds of the
obvious answer to them. **Direction:** `pressuresOn` also gives the exchange
odds for an attack the counsel might urge, from the battle's own arithmetic.

### Loop 2's structural problems, built

- **L5:** when a declaration begins work that runs over turns, the reconcile
  pass is handed each order's timing (`TIMING: … completes in N turns, not
  now`) and checks the tense, even when nothing else diverged. Cost: one Haiku
  call, about $0.003, on such a declaration.
- **L8:** the counsellor may add a `recommendation`: one sentence, one act, no
  "then", no list, held to the plan and fourth-wall guards. It is shown as
  *"Recommended: …"* under the counsel.
- **L9:** `pressuresOn` gives the odds of attacking each adjacent base of a
  power that dislikes the player (regard −25 or worse), from the exchange's own
  arithmetic, and says when the defender never breaks off. On loop 2's turn-8
  board it flags the attack on Kalzir at 1.0:1, *"both lines would be spent and
  nothing taken"*: the fight the counsel then urged, and which went exactly
  that way.

None was verified live; loop 3 is that check.

## Loop 3

Campaign `loop3`, on the build with loop 2's fixes. It cost **$2.39**, and
about **seven** new findings came out of it, against nine and about sixteen.

### Fixes that held, live

- **Timing.** The tense pass caught a three-turn programme: *"by the time this
  phase of work is done in three turns"*.
- **The force was named.** A declared attack wrote its force out, and the story
  stopped at the departure: *"their arrival still one turn away"*.
- **The epilogue was right about Torrek**: *"changed hands and changed back"*.

### Fixed in this pass

| finding | fix |
|---|---|
| **A ceasefire became a cession.** The Vigil's ceasefire named Torrek and Kalzir in `systems` (where it would hold quiet), the engine built a cession of both, and the refused cession sank the whole accord | `Concession.about` names worlds a term merely concerns; `systems` is only worlds handed over (persona prompt says so, with an example); and a cession is built only from worlds the ceder holds |
| **The odds line misled.** It fired for a world with two lifters ("0.0:1"), and ignored the defender's ships a jump away: a "2.0:1" attack met nine battleships moved in from Vantic and was mutual ruin | no line under half a battleship of weight; the defender's ships within a jump of the target are counted; the line says these are the odds of *your* attack |
| **No recommendation, ever.** The counsellor gave one in no turn of ten | required whenever the leader asks a question (`RecommendationSchema`, schema-honesty case added) |
| **The counsel flip-flopped.** It urged defending Torrek, then abandoning it, then attacking from it, on three turns running | the last counsel and recommendation ride in the session snapshot (`lastCounsel`, not world state) and are put back in front of the counsellor, told to hold to them or say what changed |
| **Ownership misread.** "The Iron Vigil holds Corvid" was said of a Meridian world with one Vigil freighter in orbit | the flags open with *"Worlds you hold: …; a rival's ships in an orbit do not make the world theirs"* |

### Still open

- **Officer pronouns.** The counsellor still writes "his" and "she" of generated
  officers, despite the prompt rule. Only a check in code would hold it, and
  telling a pronoun about an officer from one about a leader is not reliable in
  prose.
- **Reactions answer an attack with instant moves.** A defender's reaction moves
  a jump within its own space land in the batch that answers the attack, so the
  defender always gets a free reinforcement. It is arguably fair (the attacker
  is seen coming), and it is now priced into the odds line rather than changed.
